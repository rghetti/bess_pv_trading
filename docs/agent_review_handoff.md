**PV and battery auction trading implementation and review brief**

We are developing `bess_pv_trading`, a Python research pipeline for a photovoltaic farm with a lithium-ion battery sharing one grid connection in Italy's NORD bidding zone. Please review the mathematical formulation, information timing, physical assumptions, settlement accounting and experimental design described below. We want to identify assumptions that could inflate profit, hide infeasibility or introduce information from the future, and then prioritize useful extensions. The code implements a working research experiment; equipment assumptions and historical information availability still need independent validation.

The implementation has progressed from data inspection and acquisition to an offline, event-driven auction replay using actual GME prices, weather-driven PV forecasts, measured generation and actual Terna imbalance prices. The principal real-data example is 1 January 2025 for farm 910. Its price forecasts have perfect foresight, while its PV predictions use the latest eligible weather forecast at each auction. It is therefore a benchmark with perfect price information, not a historical demonstration of an executable forecasting strategy.

**Repository and configuration.** Source is under `src/bess_pv_trading/`. Configuration is split between `configs/api_experiments/`, `configs/price_experiments/` and `configs/full_experiments/`. YAML includes and nested overrides allow studies to share settings. Battery size, symmetric charge/discharge rating, SOC percentages, efficiencies, starting SOC, optional terminal SOC, POI import/export ratings, study dates, market clocks, forecasting assumptions, degradation parameters, solver settings and output paths are externally configurable. Invalid legacy battery fields and obsolete commercial trading-cap fields are rejected rather than silently ignored. The relative Gurobi MIP gap defaults to `1e-4`, or 0.01%; no absolute MIP gap is configured. The last committed revision before the subsequent POI and plotting corrections is `81d04ae`; those corrections are currently local changes.

**Data and provenance.** The market catalog is `data/api/market.duckdb`. API acquisition, imported workbooks and dataset readers use DuckDB snapshots with request metadata, raw responses, parsed records and checksums. Downloads can resume from cache and run month first. Provider credentials are supplied through the environment or private CLI prompts. Retrieval timestamps do not establish when historical market participants could have observed a record. ENTSO-E and Terna adapters exist, but provider coverage and publication vintages must be checked for each proposed study. Current development uses local data and requires no new Terna calls.

Manual GME workbooks supply NORD day-ahead and MI-A1, MI-A2 and MI-A3 prices for January through August 2025. The replay uses signed incremental energy trades and assumes price taking with guaranteed full execution. There are no bid-price curves, acceptance probabilities or rejection variables. A fully absent or cancelled auction is skipped, including its optimization variables; an incomplete price curve fails validation. Terna monthly imbalance workbooks are imported as native 15-minute NORD prices. They retain quarterly alignment and daylight-saving handling rather than being averaged to hourly prices. The imported final settlement price combines the components identified in the workbook schema. Its applicability to our hypothetical asset and market representation should still be reviewed against the relevant Italian settlement rules.

The farm archive contains four anonymous plants, with historical quarter-hour measurements and weather forecasts indexed by farm, cycle time and lead time. Weather cycles normally occur every six hours. The configured working publication delay is four hours after cycle time. Hourly interval-mean irradiance is redistributed conservatively over quarter-hours; instantaneous meteorological variables are aligned to the interval midpoint without bridging structural forecast gaps. Missing values, negative values, nighttime values, plateaus, review exclusions and treatment provenance have explicit data-policy support. The current full GME replay uses raw measured `power` for execution after finite/nonnegative checks and its configured missing-data fallback; this is distinct from applying every available historical cleaning transformation.

**PV prediction.** We use pvlib rather than directly mapping forecast irradiance to measured plant power. Inputs include forecast global horizontal irradiance, forecast horizontal direct irradiance, air temperature and wind components. Direct normal and diffuse horizontal irradiance are derived under explicit interpretation and low-sun rules. Solar position and fixed or tracking geometry determine plane-of-array irradiance. Temperature uses the Faiman model with configured coefficients and wind-height assumptions. PVWatts estimates DC power, followed by configurable PV output and transformer efficiencies to obtain predicted AC power. The current example uses 0.96 for PV output conversion and 1.0 for the additional transformer efficiency, with no separately specified AC clipping rating. These are declared study assumptions, not a fitted plant model.

Farm 910 is the largest plant in the current metadata, with a nameplate value of 6.638 MW and monoaxial tracking. The code interprets this nameplate as DC and uses Milan proxy coordinates and assumed tracker geometry. However, `AGENTS.md` describes total installed power as AC, while the adapter provenance labels the capacity DC. This source-contract inconsistency needs resolution before interpreting PV conversion losses or calling the modeled nameplate a verified electrical rating. Bifacial farm 901 currently has only an explicitly enabled front-side approximation. Snow, soiling, outages, rear-side irradiance and plant-specific calibration remain extensions. Measured production may already include curtailment or outages, so it is not necessarily unconstrained available PV generation.

At each auction, the forecast selector chooses the latest cycle available by the buffered decision cutoff. DA and IDA1 can therefore share a cycle; IDA2 and IDA3 may use newer cycles. On 1 January, the retained rolling-forecast run used three distinct cycles across four decisions. IDA3 updates the remaining physical horizon, including the interval needed to project the next state boundary, while its new financial trades are restricted to eligible afternoon products. Forecast revisions cannot rewrite past execution, earlier commitments or realized battery state. Cycle, availability and cutoff provenance are saved with the plans and execution. A cycle released after the cutoff is excluded even if its source cycle has already started.

**Market decisions and information.** There are four actionable optimization steps when all auctions occur: one immediately before DA, IDA1, IDA2 and IDA3. Each solve considers the remaining physical horizon and remaining market opportunities. Only the current auction's quantities are submitted. Future auction quantities are contingent plans and remain free when those auctions are actually reached. Previously submitted quantities become immutable immediately, including the period when their clearing price is not yet published. Publication books their realized cash flow exactly once. Publication itself does not add an optimization in this experiment. Earlier contractual cash is a sunk constant outside the residual objective; saved initial expectations and realized accounting are tracked separately.

The stochastic optimizer uses explicit information nodes. Trade, battery-control and curtailment variables are shared where scenario histories are indistinguishable, so a scenario index alone cannot reveal future observations. Scenario probabilities weight the objective. The current real-data example has one deterministic PV forecast trajectory per decision and the same actual future auction price curves throughout the price oracle. It draws no Gaussian price noise and does not have an operational weather ensemble. A new weather vintage is an updated prediction, not an additional probabilistic member.

**Variables, balances and objective.** Let `t` identify a delivery interval of duration `h_t` hours, `s` a scenario, and `a` an open auction. Positive signed trade `v_(a,t,s)` is a sale; negative is a purchase. Let `q_fixed,t` include all previously accepted and pending submitted quantities. The final contracted energy is

$$
q_{t,s}=q_{\mathrm{fixed},t}+\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm{open}}}v_{a,t,s}.
$$

Free physical variables are charge power `c`, discharge power `d`, curtailment `k`, net grid injection `g`, stored energy `E` and the binary preventing simultaneous charge/discharge. Their information-node ownership restricts the decisions that can differ by scenario. Forecast PV `p` is an input. All bus powers are on the AC side, with positive `g` meaning export:

$$
g_{t,s}=p_{t,s}-k_{t,s}+d_{t,s}-c_{t,s},\qquad
0\le k_{t,s}\le p_{t,s}.
$$

In the active study, `trading.optimize_imbalances: false` removes optimization imbalance variables, their binary directions, settlement revenue and imbalance penalties. Forecast delivery must match the final contracted energy in every quarter-hour and scenario:

$$q_{t,s}=h_tg_{t,s}.$$

With the throughput degradation model, the residual objective is

$$
\max\sum_s w_s\left[
\sum_{a,t}\pi_{a,t,s}v_{a,t,s}
-c_{\mathrm{thr}}\sum_t(c_{t,s}+d_{t,s})h_t
-f_{\mathrm{imp}}\sum_t\max(-g_{t,s},0)h_t
\right].
$$

The implementation models the import-cost term exactly when its coefficient is nonzero; the current coefficient is zero. Prices are EUR/MWh and trade variables are MWh, so auction revenue has no second duration multiplier. There is no explicit curtailment penalty. Planned curtailment has the opportunity cost implied by prices and the balance equations. A separate optional formulation supports signed optimized imbalances, but it is inactive in this study because deliberate imbalance trading was excluded by design.

**POI bounds and the removed commercial caps.** The earlier examples imposed 0.25 MWh per native product per auction and 0.25 MWh cumulative position per quarter-hour. The latter was an artificial 1 MW ceiling, separate from the farm's POI. Both configuration parameters and their model fields have now been removed. Under the latest agreed rule, cumulative positions after every modeled auction obey

$$
-P_{\mathrm{imp}}^{\mathrm{POI}}h_t
\le q_{\mathrm{fixed},t}+\sum_{b\le a}v_{b,t,s}
\le P_{\mathrm{exp}}^{\mathrm{POI}}h_t.
$$

Physical grid power also obeys `-P_POI,imp <= g <= P_POI,exp`. Thus the only trading-exposure ratings are the configured POI powers, converted using the actual interval duration. Individual adjustments have no separate volume cap: buying to close and reverse an existing sale can exceed one interval's POI energy while the resulting cumulative position remains admissible. For the farm-910 YAML, import and export are each 6.638 MW. This cumulative bound is necessary to keep unrestricted price-oracle financial round trips finite: without a financial bound, arbitrarily large offsetting trades can earn arbitrarily large revenue while leaving physical delivery unchanged.

**Battery physics and endpoints.** The active battery has nominal energy 5 MWh and one symmetric AC charge/discharge rating of 2.5 MW. SOC limits are 10% and 90% of nominal capacity, so resizing capacity automatically resizes stored-energy bounds. Initial SOC is 50%. The terminal SOC setting accepts `null`, which omits the terminal equality; the current study uses that setting. No separate battery inverter power cap remains.

For interval retention `r_t=(1-sigma)^(h_t)`, the physical transition is

$$
E_{t+1,s}=r_tE_{t,s}+\eta_c c_{t,s}h_t
-\frac{d_{t,s}h_t}{\eta_d}.
$$

The active combined charge and discharge efficiencies are each 0.97, and hourly self-discharge `sigma` is zero. Charge/discharge exclusivity and the SOC box apply in every modeled interval and scenario. The AC-coupled battery may charge from PV or imported grid energy and discharge to the grid. It shares the grid connection with PV but has its own configurable power rating. A free terminal SOC allows use of initial stored energy: the retained example ends at the minimum SOC and draws down 2 MWh of internal stock. Its one-day profit therefore includes the value of that stock depletion without an explicit terminal-energy value. Multiday carryover or a consistent initial/final energy valuation is needed for comparisons that interpret profit as repeatable daily income.

**Execution under forecast error.** The selected execution policy tracks the committed net grid position rather than blindly repeating the planned battery command. Every quarter-hour uses measured AC PV, the actual carried SOC, the current auction plan and the contractual grid target. When PV is short, planned curtailment is released first. The controller then reduces charging or increases discharge to compensate; with excess PV it reduces discharge or increases charging before adding curtailment. Its battery correction respects power, SOC and efficiency constraints.

With retained beginning energy `E_ret=r_t E_t`, the energy-feasible power limits are

$$
\bar c_t=\min\left(P_B,\frac{(E_{\max}-E_{\mathrm{ret}})_+}{\eta_ch_t}\right),\qquad
\bar d_t=\min\left(P_B,\frac{\eta_d(E_{\mathrm{ret}}-E_{\min})_+}{h_t}\right).
$$

For realized PV `p_real`, adjusted curtailment `k_adj` and target grid power `g_target`, signed requested battery injection is `g_target - p_real + k_adj`. Clipping it to `[-c_bar, d_bar]` defines feasible charging or discharging. Remaining surplus can be curtailed; remaining shortage becomes an actual delivery deviation. POI protection also applies. Actual energy and actual degradation are recomputed from the executed commands and carried into later decisions. If a forecast update and earlier immutable commitments make strict future delivery infeasible, the optimizer reports infeasibility rather than resetting SOC or inserting undeclared imbalance slack.

This feedback is an idealized response to the completed interval's average realized PV. The planning information is causal, but the controller does not simulate sub-quarter-hour telemetry, ramp dynamics or within-interval SOC excursions. Four missing nighttime actual values in the retained January example use the explicitly configured initial forecast fallback and are excluded from the corresponding error metrics. These assumptions should be distinguished from measured hardware performance.

**Degradation economics.** The active linear throughput charge uses executed AC charging plus discharging. Given replacement cost `C_rep`, reference cycle life `N`, cycle depth fraction `D`, nominal internal capacity `E_nom` and efficiencies, its normalization is

$$
c_{\mathrm{thr}}=
\frac{C_{\mathrm{rep}}}{NDE_{\mathrm{nom}}(1/\eta_c+\eta_d)}.
$$

One reference cycle traverses `D E_nom` of internal energy in each direction, consuming `D E_nom/eta_c` of AC charging and delivering `D E_nom eta_d` of AC discharge. The configured replacement proxy combines a global 2024 lithium-ion pack benchmark of USD 115/kWh with the 31 December 2024 ECB rate of USD 1.0389/EUR. The cycle-life proxy is 2,500 cycles at 80% DoD to at least 80% retained capacity from a Victron Lithium NG specification. This produces approximately EUR 553,470 replacement cost and EUR 27.6607 per MWh of total AC throughput for this 5 MWh battery. These heterogeneous references do not identify an installed utility-scale replacement quotation or the lifetime of this hypothetical battery. Labor, balance of system, financing, calendar aging and capacity fade feedback are absent. See the [BNEF benchmark](https://about.bnef.com/insights/commodities/lithium-ion-battery-pack-prices-see-largest-drop-since-2017-falling-to-115-per-kilowatt-hour-bloombergnef/), [ECB reference rate](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/exchange/eurofxref/shared/pdf/2024/12/20241231.pdf) and [Victron specification](https://www.victronenergy.com/media/pg/Lithium_NG_battery_51%2C2_V/en/technical-data.html).

A Xu-style alternative is implemented and tested but inactive. It approximates a convex depth stress function with piecewise marginal aging costs and carries segment memory between residual solves and actual execution. Charging restores depth memory; its configured cycle-accounting convention assigns aging to discharge. Priority constraints prevent selecting an artificially cheap segment inconsistent with that memory. The implementation includes an independent dispatch evaluator and rainflow comparison cases. Its stress curve, replacement cost and chemistry need calibration consistent with the throughput model before comparing economic conclusions. The current Xu implementation requires zero self-discharge. The underlying research is [Xu et al., Factoring the Cycle Aging Cost of Batteries Participating in Electricity Markets](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.04567v2).

**Actual settlement and expected-versus-realized revenue.** After replay, each quarterly imbalance is recomputed as `delta_t = h_t g_real,t - q_committed,t`. With the study's single-price settlement assumption, actual imbalance cash flow is `sum(pi_imb,t delta_t)`. A positive price remunerates excess delivery and charges short delivery; it does not imply both signs are always penalized. Negative prices reverse those cash-flow signs. Actual settlement prices are supplied only after execution and are not used in optimization. Net realized operating profit is auction cash flow plus actual imbalance cash flow minus executed battery wear and configured import fees. Expected-versus-realized mismatches are saved for gross auction cash flow and final net profit, using the initial auction solve as the baseline. Signed differences are realized minus expected, and zero baselines yield null percentages. Later PV revisions and decisions can change realized auction revenue even when all price predictions are oracle prices.

**Historical resolution and plots.** The experiment retains native hourly pre-October 2025 financial products while using quarter-hour physical energy transitions and settlement. The configured pre-October policy also ties planned battery charging and discharging power within each clock hour. This is a deliberate modeling restriction whose necessity should be reviewed independently of the hourly price-product convention: hourly prices alone do not establish that real battery operation must be flat. The execution feedback can still adjust every quarter-hour. Daylight-saving days retain their actual 92 or 100 quarters and distinguish repeated clock hours.

Delivery plots now use explicit interval starts and ends, so the last hourly or quarter-hour power/price interval reaches the available next-day 00:00 boundary. Planned and realized SOC plots include their actual final stored-energy state. Cumulative settlement includes the final interval's cash flow at its end. Missing intervals remain gaps. The generic dispatch plot uses actual interval durations when converting MW to MWh, rather than multiplying every row by 0.25. These drawing endpoints do not add measurements or extra energy to the data.

**Price forecasting beyond the active oracle.** Separate workflows implement chronological price regressions and scenario generation. Features include calendar terms, previous-day/week prices and profile summaries, with optional regional load, solar and wind forecasts subject to availability rules. Intraday models can condition spreads on earlier auctions. Training, validation, calibration and test periods remain separated in time, with refits and residual updates restricted to matured historical labels. Gaussian price noise can use a within-day covariance matrix with shrinkage and numerical regularization; complete-trajectory residual bootstrap alternatives also exist. Branch counts, random seeds, scenario probabilities and maximum tree size are configurable. With multiple unresolved auctions, price branches multiply through the tree; there is no single fixed scenarios-per-member count. Duplicate observable outcomes are merged so branch indices do not create information. This infrastructure and its tests do not establish out-of-sample trading skill or verified historical publication vintages.

**Retained results and their limits.** Two farm-910 grid-feedback results are retained: the earlier fixed-PV-vintage baseline and the later rolling-vintage replay. Both precede removal of the commercial trading caps. The rolling run has four optimal auction solves and three PV forecast cycles. It reports approximately EUR 1,469.91 net operating profit, EUR 1,745.95 auction cash flow, EUR 275.54 executed degradation, EUR -0.50 imbalance settlement, 0.0631 MWh absolute imbalance and 5.7690 MWh curtailment. Actual PV energy is approximately 16.2347 MWh. Initial expected profit is approximately EUR 1,487.58, so realized profit is approximately EUR 17.67 lower. The retained fixed-vintage run reports approximately EUR 1,486.25 profit and 0.1800 MWh absolute imbalance. Rolling forecasts reduced imbalance on this day but did not improve either PV MAE or profit. These observations concern one day with perfect future auction prices, the old trading caps and free terminal stock depletion. They are not results for the corrected POI-only trading bounds. No new historical optimization was launched for the latest POI and plotting changes.

**Artifacts and validation.** Full studies group output files under `outputs/full_experiments/gme_oracle/<run>/farm_<id>/<date>/`: `plots/`, `csv/`, `json/` and `models/`, with strategy subfolders where needed. Saved artifacts include effective YAML-derived settings, price and PV curves, each auction's expected dispatch and SOC, the immutable trade ledger, realized trajectory, imbalance settlement, degradation, revenue mismatches, model sizes, solver settings, statuses, objective bounds, validation reports and run manifests. Forecast vintages and cutoff metadata are recorded per decision and relevant interval. Earlier experiment outputs were removed at the user's request; raw data, DuckDB and acquisition/import provenance remain available. Older studies may require regeneration of deleted intermediate model artifacts.

The independent numerical validator reconstructs contractual positions and cash flow and checks power balances, SOC transitions, efficiency application, exclusivity, physical limits, cumulative POI-derived positions, settlement and degradation. Regression cases cover information timing, pending commitments, cancelled auctions, native product equalities, asymmetric POI ratings, reversal trades, hourly/quarter-hour durations, DST, forecast updates, execution feedback and final plotting boundaries. The complete suite passed 706 tests after the POI and plotting corrections. Passing tests establish the checked implementation contracts, not the economic validity of the assumptions or actual market performance.

**Review priorities and extensions.** First resolve the AC/DC nameplate inconsistency and validate the price-taking, cumulative-position and Italian settlement assumptions for the intended asset representation. Rerun the one-day comparison under the corrected POI bounds before drawing further conclusions from curtailment or arbitrage. Separate physical energy revenue, financial auction spread revenue, initial-energy stock depletion, forecast error and degradation in that comparison. Evaluate whether hourly battery-dispatch equality is an intended restriction or an unnecessarily conservative approximation.

Then extend to multiple delivery days with consistent carried SOC and energy valuation; compare PV-only, forecast-driven battery control, fixed-vintage control, rolling-vintage control and genuine PV-and-price perfect foresight under the same constraints. Calibrate plant-specific PV geometry, output conversion, temperature and availability using causally eligible training data. Replace illustrative replacement/lifetime economics with consistent utility-scale equipment references and sensitivity ranges. Validate forecast quality and economic results over seasonal periods with chronological splits and verified publication timing. Finally, introduce calibrated weather ensembles, price/PV dependence, downside-risk controls, finer execution dynamics, and more detailed degradation only where the data and study question support them. Continuous intraday trading, bid acceptance, ancillary services, portfolio allocation and market impact remain outside the current experiment.
