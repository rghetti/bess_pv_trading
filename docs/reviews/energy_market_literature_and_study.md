# Energy-market research directions and literature

This note maps the implemented continuous auction replay to closely related research. “Continuous” below means chronological auction reoptimization and physical MPC, not trading in a continuous order book. The historical replay is retrospective: several source release clocks are assumptions, and reviewed PV target replacements are counterfactual. Its current short January and August windows are development evidence, not a basis for claiming a general empirical result.

1. **Forecast quality versus economic value.** [Hirsch and Ziel (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19580) study how probabilistic electricity-price forecast evaluation and storage trading outcomes can disagree and discuss the importance of intertemporal dependence. Here, chronological price-only and fundamentals ridge models and seasonal persistence are evaluated using MAE and empirical CRPS; the continuous replay also compares selected price-only and fundamentals controllers. The 2024 day-ahead history and coarse hourly intraday history are training inputs; the settlement history starts in 2025. The implementation does not claim that the best CRPS model is the best trading policy, and its retrospective publication-clock assumptions remain explicit.

2. **Multiperiod price uncertainty and risk.** [Weron and Maciejowska](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26122) study multidimensional probabilistic electricity-price forecasts for BESS trading and compare both distributional and economic scores. The present library samples empirical daily settlement residual paths and offers monetary operating-loss CVaR in physical MPC. Auction clearing forecasts are currently point curves and PV-price coupling is an explicit independence assumption. A coherent joint model of DA/IDA/imbalance spreads, temporal dependence and PV dependence remains unimplemented; the present workflow cannot demonstrate its value.

3. **Coherent PV ensembles and adaptive recourse.** [Schefzik, Thorarinsdottir and Gneiting (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.7149) introduce ensemble copula coupling: calibrate marginal distributions and reorder samples using the raw ensemble rank structure. This library compares raw, calibrated ECC and independently shuffled trajectories with the same calibrated marginal samples. Its adaptive MPC branches future actions on past PV histories, with a common first action and a disclosure lag. Its affine calibration is a small baseline, not a replication of every ECC postprocessing method. More 2026 members/cycles can be added through source inventory and dynamic member counts; forecast-score and trading results should be reported separately.

4. **Information updates and market access.** [Sohrabi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03763) formulate sequential day-ahead and intraday auction decisions under uncertainty in a charging-station setting; their asset and market model differs from this project. The present four-cell study compares updated versus frozen-initial forecast information and DA-only versus DA-plus-IDA auction access, always with live continuous commitments. It estimates a matched difference-in-differences, not the value of actual continuous-order-book access. Real order-book trading requires historical quotes, spreads, liquidity and execution assumptions not present in the auction-price data.

5. **Forecast quality and physical flexibility.** Comparing raw and calibrated scenarios at baseline and changed battery energy, power or POI ratings tests whether a forecast change is more valuable under some physical constraints. The reporting checks that raw/calibrated cases within each asset setting match and that only the declared asset fields differ across settings. Interaction estimates condition on the chosen cases and dates; they are not a causal estimate for unobserved assets, and proxy POI/battery specifications remain assumptions.

6. **Continuation value and end-of-horizon behavior.** [Zheng, Jaworski and Xu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06000) use a piecewise-linear value-to-go in stochastic storage arbitrage with Markov prices. This library's terminal curve is a nominal forecast-conditioned dynamic program with an explicit energy grid, not that paper's stochastic Markov algorithm. It omits Xu cycle-memory effects in the continuation approximation and does not value unmodeled future auctions. Report terminal battery energy alongside profit because terminal treatments differ between controllers.

7. **Decision-aware reduction.** [Zhuang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07810) formulate problem-driven scenario reduction for stochastic power-system operation and explicitly relate reduction to downstream optimization. The current implementation is a weighted medoid heuristic using PV, ramps, cumulative energy imbalance, physical limits and economic exposure; it is not the cited mixed-integer reduction method and has no optimality-gap guarantee. Compare retained-member weights, forecast scores, runtime, physical feasibility and realized policy outcomes rather than assuming fewer scenarios preserve economic value.

The research matrix and its limitations are specified in `docs/energy_market_research_extensions.md`; completed evidence is indexed in `docs/reviews/energy_research_results_index.md`. The reviewed farm-910 August targets are wholly reconstructed or night-zero, so they cannot independently validate forecast accuracy. A larger 2026 ensemble archive and the user's forthcoming PV models are needed before drawing broad conclusions from seasonal or farm comparisons.
