# First-stage ensemble MPC performance investigation

A recorded difficult first MPC decision for farm 1 on 2 August 2026 was reconstructed from the completed legacy replay's 51 PV members, 96 quarter-hours, actual starting SOC and already-submitted auction positions. The comparison varies only the first-stage minimum expected absolute imbalance problem. Prices do not enter that objective. All tests used one Gurobi thread, a 10-second optimization limit and relative MIP gap 1e-4; results depend on machine load and are a single-instance diagnostic, not a general benchmark.

| Continuous relaxation | 10,015 | 0 | 6,675 | 4.068396 | 4.068396 | — | Optimal LP in 0.24 s; 20 intervals charge and discharge simultaneously |

Gurobi `MIPFocus` values 1, 2 and 3 did not improve the reported incumbent or bound in this instance. The epigraph version reduces variables but adds constraints and did not reduce the gap here; it remains a benchmark experiment, not the production formulation. The LP relaxation is a lower bound, not a valid physical dispatch: 36 mode variables are fractional and 20 intervals charge and discharge at once. Dropping the charge/discharge binary would change the feasible physical behavior.

The solver sees an imbalance objective alone in the oracle's first stage. The ordinary ensemble controller minimizes weighted absolute imbalance at 500 EUR/MWh plus wear and a soft terminal inventory penalty. In the oracle, stage two maximizes signed settlement less costs only after the first stage meets its configured gap, subject to its imbalance budget. When the first stage times out with a feasible incumbent, that price-blind incumbent is used and stage two is skipped. In the legacy four-farm August development runs, 40 of 192 decisions per farm reached this limit for both deterministic and ensemble oracle cases; the ordinary farm-1 ensemble controller's median solve was 0.107 s, while the oracle median was 0.350 s. These results should not be generalized to new models or other windows.

An opt-in fallback accepts a first-stage incumbent only when its independently reconstructed gap relative to Gurobi's lower bound is at most 1%. An initial experiment reserved half a shared 10-second budget: stage one reached a 0.1277% gap in 4.54 s and the economic stage solved in 2.79 s. It respected the incumbent imbalance cap to numerical tolerance. Its scenario-weighted, oracle-price settlement projection was EUR 571.995 versus EUR 413.071 for the original feasible first-stage plan, while projected wear was essentially unchanged. This is a single horizon projection, not realized profit or a general performance estimate. Following the later study request, the configured comparisons now give each stage an independent 10-second solver limit. On this instance the first stage used 10.00 s, the second solved in 1.72 s and the reconstructed imbalance was 4.098331871457 MWh versus a cap of 4.098331871456 MWh. Model building and validation add wall time. The fallback remains disabled by default outside its explicit study cases.

Machine-readable details and the exact reconstruction command are in `mpc_stage1_august_benchmark.json`, `mpc_stage1_epigraph_benchmark.json`, `mpc_stage1_relaxation_diagnostic.json`, `mpc_stage1_gap_fallback_benchmark.json`, `mpc_stage1_two_stage_10s_final.json` and `scripts/benchmark_mpc_stage1.py`. The first-stage baseline and variants have the same integer feasible set for the recorded throughput-wear, no-hard-terminal case; the continuous relaxation deliberately does not. The production solver retains its validated integer constraints.
