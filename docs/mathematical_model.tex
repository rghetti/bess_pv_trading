\begin{abstract}
This document describes the implemented optimization and execution system for a
single photovoltaic farm and an AC-coupled battery sharing one point of
interconnection. It distinguishes auction plans, immutable market commitments,
physical feedback execution, and ex-post settlement. The shared Gurobi formulation
supports a risk-neutral scenario tree. The current historical January experiment
instead uses one deterministic rolling weather forecast and perfect auction-price
information, followed by execution against measured AC production. A separate
continuous experiment applies daily planning chronologically while carrying
actual battery state, outstanding orders and operator fee history across days. Separate
full-information PV+BESS and PV-only optimizations quantify an ex-post operating
value of storage. Equations below follow the current source code; they are not a
proposal or a claim of a fully causal historical trading strategy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and implementation map}

One farm has PV generation, an AC-coupled BESS, and a common AC grid connection.
There is no local electricity demand. PV can be exported, stored, or curtailed;
the battery can charge from PV or grid imports and discharge toward the grid.
The market sequence is MGP (called DA in code), MI-A1, MI-A2, and MI-A3 (IDA1,
IDA2, IDA3). Every submitted signed quantity is assumed to execute in full at the
auction clearing price. Bid prices, acceptance uncertainty, market impact, and
continuous intraday trading are outside this formulation.

The implementation has three distinct objects:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An optimization plan, which includes the current actionable auction order,
future auction recourse, and a battery/PV schedule over the remaining horizon.
\item An immutable commitment ledger, updated at submission and publication.
\item A physical trajectory produced by a feedback/protection policy using
realized interval-average PV. Its energy, wear, and deviations are settled
separately from the optimization objective.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Time, units, signs and information}

\subsection{Delivery intervals and auction products}
A local delivery day is partitioned into physical intervals
$\mathcal T=\{0,\ldots,T-1\}$, with starts $\tau_t$, ends $\tau_{t+1}$ and durations
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_t=(\tau_{t+1}-\tau_t)/\text{one hour}.
\end{equation}
The historical study uses quarter-hours, so $\Delta_t=0.25$. A local day is
constructed between successive midnights in \texttt{Europe/Rome}, then stored in
UTC: $T$ is 92, 96, or 100 for spring-DST, ordinary, or autumn-DST days. Repeated
local hours are distinguished by their actual UTC offset/fold. No equation
assumes that every day has exactly 24 elapsed hours.

A physical interval is not necessarily an independent market product. Before
1 October 2025, DA products are hourly; the MI auctions in the current 2025
history are already quarter-hourly. Physical battery controls are quarter-hourly
at all four optimization stages. For the four equal-duration quarters of one
native DA hour $h$, the implementation enforces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hourly}
 q_{\mathrm{DA},n,t}=q_{\mathrm{DA},n,t'}\quad(t,t'\in h).
\end{equation}
These are equal MWh shares of one hourly order, whose total is
$\sum_{t\in h}q_{\mathrm{DA},n,t}$. The hourly clearing price is repeated as an
objective coefficient on those shares. The quarters do not become separate DA
trades or separate observed prices. There is no corresponding date-based
hourly equality on battery charging or discharging. Native product resolution
and optional physical-control grouping are separate configuration fields.

\subsection{Forecast vintages and observability}
A weather forecast cycle $v$ has source time $t_v$ and availability time $A_v$.
Without explicit source publication times, the configured convention is
$A_v=t_v+6$ hours. At decision cutoff $k$, the rolling policy chooses the latest
eligible cycle with $A_v\le k$ and required remaining-horizon coverage. Weather
cycles about six hours apart are successive forecast vintages, not ensemble
members. The current dataset supplies one deterministic weather member.

Measured interval-average PV becomes available after interval end plus configured
telemetry delay. A forecast for future intervals is not replaced by future
realized power in the ordinary auction replay. If a decision occurs inside an
unfinished quarter-hour, the next boundary state is projected through that
interval with forecast PV and the same execution policy. Its final realized
average cannot yet be observed. The hindsight counterfactuals deliberately have
future PV and price information and are labeled as such.

Policy-treated observations carry evidence times. Plateau evidence can require
later samples; two-sided interpolation requires its right endpoint and applicable
mask evidence. A finalized corrected time series therefore differs from the
as-of view available to a past decision. This remains relevant even when a study
uses actual-price foresight.

\section{PV power from weather forecasts}

\subsection{Irradiance and orientation}
The input mappings declare forecast shortwave radiation as GHI and direct
radiation as horizontal beam irradiance (BHI), with the latter an explicit
working assumption. Hourly radiation means are end-labelled and redistributed
uniformly over contained quarters. Other fields follow their declared
instantaneous/accumulation semantics rather than the same blanket interpolation.
Solar geometry is evaluated at each quarter-hour midpoint using configured
coordinates and farm elevation.

For eligible irradiance data,
\begin{equation}
 G_{\rm DHI}=G_{\rm GHI}-G_{\rm BHI},\qquad
 G_{\rm DNI}=\frac{G_{\rm BHI}}{\cos\theta_z}
 \quad\text{away from the low-sun cutoff}.
\end{equation}
The code uses pvlib's DNI helper with configured low-sun handling and a finite
DNI cap or invalidation rule. BHI exceeding GHI is either invalidated or explicitly
clipped and flagged. Optional Erbs decomposition can replace missing/unusable
direct input under its declared conditions; it is not a general repair of
contradictory source values.

Fixed arrays use metadata tilt and a declared source-to-pvlib azimuth conversion.
Tracking IDs 1 and 3 use \texttt{pvlib.tracking.singleaxis} with configurable axis
tilt, axis azimuth, rotation limit, backtracking and ground-coverage ratio. Type
3 defaults to an explicit front-side-only approximation. The optional
infinite-sheds route models front/rear irradiance when bifacial modeling is enabled. The current geometry uses an acknowledged Milan
location proxy rather than verified farm coordinates.

With panel tilt $\beta$, incidence angle $\alpha$ and ground albedo $a$, the
isotropic transposition can be written
\begin{equation}
 G_{\rm POA}=G_{\rm DNI}\pos{\cos\alpha}
   +G_{\rm DHI}\frac{1+\cos\beta}{2}
   +aG_{\rm GHI}\frac{1-\cos\beta}{2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Temperature, DC output and AC conversion}
Wind speed is the magnitude of the configured horizontal wind components,
$v=\sqrt{v_u^2+v_v^2}$. The Faiman temperature model is
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm mod}=T_{\rm air}+\frac{G_{\rm POA}}{U_0+U_1 v}.
\end{equation}
The supplied wind height is explicitly acknowledged as a module-wind proxy.
The AC farm rating is converted to a DC reference rating by dividing by
the fixed, configurable assumption $\eta_{\rm ref}$, currently 0.96.
This assumption is independent of the downstream DC-to-AC coefficient.
The resulting temperature and DC
reference rating are passed to pvlib's PVWatts DC calculation:
\begin{equation}
 P_{\rm DC}=\max\left\{0,\ \frac{P_{\rm AC,rated}}{\eta_{\rm ref}}
 \frac{G_{\rm POA}}{1000}
 \left[1+\gamma(T_{\rm mod}-T_{\rm ref})\right]\right\}.
\end{equation}
DC power is scaled to AC by the configured explicit PV-output efficiency
$\eta_{\rm PV}$, or by an explicitly fitted DC-to-AC coefficient $\kappa$:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat P_{\rm AC}=
 \begin{cases}
 \eta_{\rm tr}\eta_{\rm PV}P_{\rm DC},&\text{explicit-efficiency route},\\
 \eta_{\rm tr}\kappa P_{\rm DC},&\text{fitted-coefficient route}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
A configured AC cap, limited to no more than the farm AC rating, clips this output;
the night mask and validity flags are then applied. Without a fitted override,
the model uses the configured fixed output and separate transformer factors.
An offline calibration pairs each quarter-hour's latest available weather
vintage with measured AC production $y_i$ and modeled DC $d_i$. With
$x_i=\eta_{\rm tr}d_i$, it estimates the zero-intercept slope by minimizing
mean squared error:
\begin{equation}
 \hat\kappa=\mathop{\arg\min}_{\kappa>0}\frac1n\sum_i(y_i-\kappa x_i)^2
 =\frac{\sum_i x_i y_i}{\sum_i x_i^2}.
\end{equation}
Low-DC and invalid observations are excluded according to the saved study
configuration. A chronological holdout compares the training-only fit with
the configured explicit coefficient; the reported final coefficient is then
refitted on all eligible pairs. This is a predictive aggregate scale, not an
identified physical inverter efficiency.
Temperature and wind affect production
through temperature; irradiance and orientation affect POA. Cloud/dew-point
fields retained in the store are not additional direct PV predictors here.

\subsection{Chronological physical PV calibration}

The \texttt{study-pv-calibration} workflow compares physical models separately
for each farm. It constructs the weather view available at each auction's
submission cutoff, including the configured six-hour UTC lag and decision
buffer. IDA3 evaluation covers only local noon onwards. Targets are raw
quarter-hour measured AC power; no transformer loss, interpolation or forecast
substitution is applied to the targets. Missing/invalid targets are excluded
by a common mask; genuine zeros remain. Satellite GHI diagnoses weather error
without entering an operational prediction.

Let $\mathcal T_{da}$ be the eligible daylight targets for delivery day $d$
and auction view $a$, and $f(X_{dat};\theta)$ the shared physical predictor,
including its AC cap and night policy. The fitted objective is
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\theta\in\mathcal B}\;
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal G|}\sum_{(d,a)\in\mathcal G}
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal T_{da}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{da}}
 \rho\!\left(\frac{f(X_{dat};\theta)-P^{\rm measured}_{dt}}
 {P_{\rm AC,rated}}\right)
 +\lambda\sum_j\left(\frac{\theta_j-\theta_{0,j}}{u_j-l_j}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Here $[l_j,u_j]$ are configured bounds, $\theta_0$ denotes the configured
assumptions, and $\rho$ is squared error or Huber loss. Equal day/auction
weights prevent the shorter IDA3 horizon being underweighted. Scale-only
fits optimize the clipped forward calculation; the earlier closed-form
slope is retained only by the legacy single-coefficient command.

Candidate families fit $\kappa$ alone, $(\kappa,U_0,U_1)$, or additionally
tracker GCR and maximum rotation for tracking types 1 and 3. The DC reference,
$\gamma$, transformer factor, known fixed-array orientation and tracker-axis
assumptions remain fixed. Front-side candidates compare isotropic, Hay--Davies
and Perez--Driesse transposition. For farm 901, an additional infinite-sheds
family uses explicit bifaciality and height/pitch assumptions, holding geometry
fixed while fitting scale and thermal coefficients. Electrical effective POA
includes the weighted rear irradiance, while Faiman uses incident front plus
rear POA. One-at-a-time rear-assumption sensitivities are reported separately.
AC power alone does not establish that fitted coefficients are true equipment
properties; bound hits, optimizer stops and local Jacobian diagnostics are saved.

January is warmup and February--December 2025 are held-out delivery months.
Monthly fits are issued at local midnight on the preceding calendar day, before
the month's first DA submission. Only already available completed days enter
training. The latest seven days select the history window (expanding or 90
days), transposition and regularization, after which the specification is
refitted on the available history. A separate selected strategy chooses among
families using that earlier validation alone. A final all-year fit is labeled
retrospective/in-sample and never included in held-out scores. Paired accuracy
intervals resample seven-day blocks; daily energy errors remain separate by
auction because auction views overlap.

The \texttt{retrain-pv-calibration} command reuses this fitting procedure at a
configured historical-availability timestamp. Immutable artifacts retain farm
metadata, training/issue cutoffs, source checksums, software versions and fixed
physical assumptions. The optional \path{pv.parameter_registry} selects only
the latest matching artifact issued by a forecast's decision cutoff, using the
fixed baseline before the first fit. This interface is shared by ordinary PV
forecasts and auction experiments and is disabled by default.

For ordinary experiments, the shared configuration now stores a final static
parameter set under each farm's \texttt{pv} mapping. The model family is chosen
by the full held-out comparison and its final all-2025 refit supplies the
numbers. By the user's modeling convention, these inferred quantities are
treated as prior farm metadata and apply at every experiment date, including
January 2025. Their full-year fit scores are in-sample. This static assumption
is distinct from the causally issued monthly benchmark; that study explicitly
disables the farm mappings to preserve its original unfitted reference.

\subsubsection{How the installed 2025 parameter sets were selected}

The completed run is
\path{outputs/pv_calibration/physical_pv_all_farms_2025/}. It contains 365
delivery days per farm, with January warmup and 334 February--December
evaluation days. It completed 312 method/issue checkpoints across the four
farms. A finite shared eligibility mask is frozen before model comparisons;
the 12 missing unique target quarters per farm are not filled or scored.
The selection has three separate levels:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For each method and monthly issue, fit on the earlier history and
validate on its latest seven completed days. At least 14 earlier training
days are required. Compare expanding and trailing 90-day history, admissible
transposition models, and $\lambda\in\{0,0.01\}$. Scale-only fits retain the
reference transposition and use $\lambda=0$. Rank candidates by daylight,
AC-normalized RMSE with equal aggregate weight per day/auction view; then
refit that candidate on its entire available history, including validation.
The Huber transition is $0.05$ of AC rating.
\item Evaluate the resulting monthly forecasts on the following delivery
month. For the static defaults, select the method family with the smallest
aggregate February--December daylight RMSE. This is a hindsight family
selection; the separate monthly \texttt{selected} strategy uses only prior
validation and can choose a different family each month.
\item For the selected family, use its final all-2025 refit. This final fit
uses expanding history only; its transposition and regularization are chosen
on the latest seven training days, and its physical parameters are then
refitted on all 365 days. These are the values installed in
\path{configs/standard.yaml}, with provenance artifacts in
\path{configs/pv_parameters/2025/}. By the agreed convention they are applied
as static prior metadata at every delivery date.
\end{enumerate}

This is bounded nonlinear physical calibration, not a regression on arbitrary
weather features. The scale-only problem uses bounded scalar minimization;
thermal/tracker/bifacial fits use L-BFGS-B in unit-box parameter coordinates.
The completed benchmark used the configured reference start plus one seeded
random start, a budget of 180 function evaluations per start, and retained
the best finite iterate, including the starting reference. The $L^2$ term
regularizes deviations from the original physical assumptions; no $L^1$
penalty is used. All four installed final fits selected $\lambda=0$.

\subsection{Independent neural PV benchmark}

The research branch \texttt{feature/neural-pv-benchmark} adds
\texttt{study-pv-neural}. It fits a distinct neural model for each farm and
compares it with the calibrated physical predictor on the same auction-time
weather and raw measured AC targets. The default chronology is 2025 training,
January--April 2026 validation, and May--August 2026 test. One configured
embargo day at each split boundary ensures fitting/selection observations
are available before the next split's first day-ahead submission. Validation
chooses epochs and candidates; test observations choose neither.

Let $X_{dat}$ contain the selected forecast weather, deterministic solar
geometry, cyclic calendar features, forecast lead and missingness indicators.
Its imputation and standardization statistics use training data only.
The original saved models augment those numeric features with learned month
and auction embeddings $e_{\theta}(m,a)$. New shared defaults disable the
auction embedding, while historical artifacts retain their saved contract.
The newer weather-only search below removes both embeddings and proxy solar
geometry. In the original workflow each farm predicts
\begin{equation}
 \widehat P_{dat}^{\rm NN}=P_{\rm AC,rated}\,D_{dt}\,\sigma\!\left(
 g_{\theta_f}(X_{da,\cdot},e_\theta(m,a))_t\right),
\end{equation}
where $D_{dt}$ is the deterministic daylight/night-policy indicator and
$\sigma(z)=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}$. This enforces nonnegative production within the
known AC rating. Capacity normalization is not an electrical conversion loss.
Targets remain unchanged measured AC; genuine daylight zeros are retained.

The core features use forecast global/horizontal-direct irradiance, air
temperature, wind and solar position, plus hour/year/month, cycle hour and
lead encodings. An extended view adds dew point, cloud layers, precipitation,
snow, visibility and higher-level wind. Neither view includes fitted output,
DC, thermal or tracker parameters, physical POA/cell-temperature intermediates,
satellite measurements, or past/future measured production. Constant farm
hardware characteristics are represented implicitly by the separate fitted
weights, with known capacity/tracking/orientation retained as provenance.

The comparison uses a dense MLP, residual MLP and temporal convolution, each
with core and extended weather. Dense layers act independently at each time;
residual blocks retain a direct hidden path; the temporal model convolves
within a complete day/auction forecast view using increasing dilations.
Its symmetric receptive field can use later forecast leads because those
weather predictions are already available at the common cutoff. It never
crosses a date, auction or weather-vintage boundary and never sees later
measurements. IDA3 retains its noon-onward eligibility and native quarter-hour
grid, including both daylight-saving transitions.

Training minimizes balanced day/auction MSE or Huber loss on AC-normalized
power using AdamW. Early stopping and candidate selection minimize prior
validation daylight RMSE. The default run trains six candidates per farm,
with deterministic seeds and CPU workers/threads controlled in

\section{Optimization at an auction decision}

Each residual solve uses only the remaining suffix of the delivery grid. In the
equations below that suffix is reindexed from zero, with $T$ denoting its final
boundary; its original UTC product identifiers are retained for commitments.

\subsection{Scenario tree and nonanticipativity}
At decision $k$, let $\mathcal S_k$ be represented leaf scenarios and let
$\mathcal A_k$ be the open active auctions. An information tree assigns an auction
bid owner $n_a(s)$ to each auction/leaf and a control owner $n_t(s)$ to each
interval/leaf. Curtailment may have a separate owner $m_t(s)$ under the selected
execution-information convention.

Decisions are node-owned variables, not independent leaf decisions followed by a
penalty for disagreement. For example,
\begin{equation}
 c_{s,t}=c_{n_t(s),t},\quad d_{s,t}=d_{n_t(s),t},\quad
 q_{a,s,t}=q_{a,n_a(s),t},\quad u_{s,t}=u_{m_t(s),t}.
\end{equation}
Thus two scenarios with the same observable history share actionable decisions.
A latent PV-member label is not available information by itself. Constraints
apply to every represented leaf, including zero-probability leaves if supplied;
probabilities only weight the objective. In the current one-scenario historical
price oracle, the tree degenerates to deterministic planning with known future
prices and a forecast PV trajectory.

\subsection{Physical variables and balances}
For every scenario and remaining interval:
\begin{align}
 i_{s,t}+u_{s,t}&=V_{s,t}, & i_{s,t}&\ge0, & 0\le u_{s,t}&\le V_{s,t},\label{eq:pvbalance}\\
 g_{s,t}&=i_{s,t}+d_{s,t}-c_{s,t},\label{eq:bus}\\
 -P^{\rm imp}\le g_{s,t}&\le P^{\rm exp}.\label{eq:poi}
\end{align}
Curtailment appears only in the PV balance. It cannot erase bought grid energy
or battery charging energy. A negative grid exchange must be absorbed by net
battery charging since there is no local demand. The model does not explicitly
assign charging power to its PV/grid source; that partition is an accounting
convention used in post-processing.

With $z_{n,t}\in\{0,1\}$ denoting charging mode,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power}
 0\le c_{n,t}\le\overline P z_{n,t},\qquad
 0\le d_{n,t}\le\overline P(1-z_{n,t}).
\end{equation}
This excludes simultaneous charge and discharge, uses one symmetric AC rating,
and introduces no separate battery-inverter power limit.

SOC percentage parameters $s_{\min},s_{\max}$ define
\begin{equation}
 E^{\min}=E\frac{s_{\min}}{100},\qquad
 E^{\max}=E\frac{s_{\max}}{100},\qquad
 E^{\min}\le e_{s,t}\le E^{\max}.
\end{equation}
For $\rho_t=(1-\sigma)^{\Delta_t}$, the transition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soc}
 e_{s,t+1}=\rho_t e_{s,t}
    +\eta_c c_{s,t}\Delta_t-\frac{d_{s,t}\Delta_t}{\eta_d}.
\end{equation}
One AC MWh charged stores $\eta_c$ MWh. Delivering one AC MWh consumes
$1/\eta_d$ internal MWh. In combined mode the two configured efficiencies each
cover their entire directional path. In converter-and-cell mode,
$\eta_c=\eta_{c,\rm conv}\eta_{c,\rm cell}$ and
$\eta_d=\eta_{d,\rm conv}\eta_{d,\rm cell}$. The alternatives cannot be mixed.
The round-trip factor is $\eta_c\eta_d$. Self-discharge acts on stored energy,
not on a power variable, and is separate from cycle degradation cost.

The initial boundary is fixed to measured/carried/projected state $e^k$:
\begin{equation}
 e_{s,0}=e^k.
\end{equation}
If the terminal target is configured, also impose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal}
 e_{s,T}=e^{\rm target}.
\end{equation}
In the GME adapter, initial/terminal percentages are converted using nominal
capacity. A \texttt{null} terminal SOC omits~\eqref{eq:terminal}. Prepared and
synthetic boundary-state contracts use explicitly named MWh values. Neither
version adds a terminal energy salvage value to the objective.
An independent optional lower target, configured by
\path{state.terminal_soc_min_percent}, imposes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal-minimum}
 e_{s,T}\ge E\,s_{\mathrm{floor}}/100.
\end{equation}
Its shared default is \texttt{null}. When enabled it applies to every planning
horizon end, including intermediate delivery days in continuous operation.
A non-null final equality and this floor both apply on the final day. The
floor constrains planned energy; forecast-error feedback can produce a
realized closing SOC below it while still respecting the hard SOC box.


\subsection{Auction quantities and physical delivery}
Let $Q^{\rm fix}_t$ contain all previously submitted commitments, priced or still
pending. The new final position on leaf $s$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:position}
 Q_{s,t}=Q^{\rm fix}_t+
       \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_k:\,t\in\mathcal T_a}q_{a,n_a(s),t}.
\end{equation}
An ineligible auction/interval has no trade variable. The model allows signed
sales/purchases; they are adjustments to prior positions, not gross generation.
For every eligible trade,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tradepoi}
 -P^{\rm imp}\Delta_t\le q_{a,n,t}\le P^{\rm exp}\Delta_t.
\end{equation}
For every open auction $a$ in chronological order, define the cumulative planned
position after that auction as
\begin{equation}
 C^a_{s,t}=Q^{\rm fix}_t+
 \sum_{b\in\mathcal A_k:\,b\le a,\,t\in\mathcal T_b}q_{b,n_b(s),t}.
\end{equation}
It satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cumulative}
 -P^{\rm imp}\Delta_t\le C^a_{s,t}\le P^{\rm exp}\Delta_t,
 \qquad -P^{\rm imp}\Delta_t\le Q_{s,t}\le P^{\rm exp}\Delta_t.
\end{equation}
There is no independent commercial volume cap. The directional POI ratings apply
to individual auction adjustments, cumulative positions after each auction, and
physical delivery. An offsetting later trade is allowed, subject to its own
POI-derived limit and the resulting position limit. Thus the model permits
bounded cross-auction spread trading even without a battery; its revenues can be
present in both asset counterfactuals.

With \texttt{trading.optimize\_imbalances: false}, which is the current farm-910
policy, planned delivery is exact in every interval:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact}
 g_{s,t}\Delta_t=Q_{s,t}.
\end{equation}
No positive/negative imbalance variables or imbalance direction binary are
created. Forecast deviations are not an additional dispatch resource.
An optional configured product-group delivery equality can additionally impose
$\sum_{t\in h}(g_{s,t}\Delta_t-Q_{s,t})=0$. It is redundant with~\eqref{eq:exact}
and does not replace it in the disabled-imbalance mode.

\subsection{Current objective and sunk cash flows}
For throughput degradation with coefficient $c_{\rm thr}$ and an optional import
fee $f$, the current residual objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
 \max J_k=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S_k}w_s\left[
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_k}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_a}\pi_{a,s,t}q_{a,n_a(s),t}
 -c_{\rm thr}\sum_t(c_{s,t}+d_{s,t})\Delta_t
 -f\sum_t\pos{-g_{s,t}}\Delta_t-C^{\rm trading}_s\right].
\end{equation}
The implementation uses an exact import-power linearization with a direction
binary only when $f$ is nonzero. The farm-910 study sets $f=0$.

\subsection{Market transaction fees and operator monthly volume}
The \texttt{transaction\_fees.mode} is \texttt{none},
\texttt{flat}, or \texttt{gme\_real} (the shared YAML default). Legacy programmatic
inputs without a fee section remain fee-free. Let $m(a)$ be the calendar month of auction
submission in market local time, $V_m$ known already executed operator volume,
and $G_{m,s}=\sum_{a:m(a)=m,t}|q_{a,n_a(s),t}|$ new gross MWh. Purchases and
sales count separately even when final delivery is zero. Quarter-hour energy
shares of an hourly product are summed once, without another duration factor.
For a cumulative progressive tariff $F_m$,
\begin{equation}
 C^{\rm trading}_s=\sum_m\left[F_m(V_m+G_{m,s})-F_m(V_m)\right].
\end{equation}
In flat mode this is the configured EUR/MWh coefficient times gross MWh.
The published GME MPE marginal rates are 0 on the first 20,000 MWh/month,
0.04 up to 1,000,000, 0.03 up to 10,000,000, and 0.02 thereafter.
These bands are progressive and not a retroactive rate on the monthly total.

Fee-free horizons introduce no auxiliary variables. Positive single-band costs
use linear absolute-value epigraphs. Across reachable breakpoints, exact Gurobi
ABS general constraints and a PWL equality represent both gross volume and the
nonconvex tariff; a convex epigraph alone is not used for regressive bands.
Absolute-value variables retain auction-node ownership and native-product
sharing. Costs are evaluated for each leaf and weighted by $w_s$.

The fee state includes pending guaranteed transactions immediately at submission.
Publication adds no second fee. Executed gross volume remains in $V_m$ during
residual solves, while incurred EUR are sunk. The state carries an availability
clock and declared provenance; nonzero future executed monthly volume is
rejected. Months use transaction dates, so pre-DA/IDA1/IDA2 and IDA3 for one
delivery day may belong to different months. A new month starts at zero.
The saved final fee state can initialize a later chronological study. Operator
scope must explicitly distinguish a standalone plant from a portfolio with
exogenous starting volume. Unknown future external activity is not invented:
costs are exact conditional on the supplied initial state, not a complete
operator-level monthly forecast.

Tariffs have explicit nonoverlapping complete-month validity windows, version,
source, thresholds, marginal rates and an applicability status. The current
\href{https://gme.mercatoelettrico.org/it-it/Home/Accesso-ai-Mercati/Elettricita/MercatiElettrici/CorrispettiviSettlement/CorrispettiviElettricita}{GME tariff}
and the \href{https://gme.mercatoelettrico.org/Portals/0/Documents/it-IT/20070502Bilancio2006.pdf}{2006 annual report}
show the same structure, the latter effective in 2007. A dated confirmation of
2025 applicability was not established; the shared December 2024--December 2026 tariff marks
this explicitly as \texttt{assumed\_unchanged}. Unsupported dates fail.
The shared initial state assumes a standalone simulated operator at local
midnight on 1 December 2024, with zero December volume, zero earlier fees and
no external portfolio activity. Subsequent months begin at zero and continuous
execution carries actual submitted volume. Known operator-level volume should
replace this assumption when the plant belongs to a larger portfolio. Enabling
this tariff can still produce zero variable fees below the monthly exemption.
Example YAMLs are
\path{configs/full_experiments/synthetic_fees_none.yaml},
\path{configs/full_experiments/synthetic_fees_flat.yaml}, and
\path{configs/full_experiments/synthetic_fees_gme.yaml}.
Membership, taxes, ACER record charges and separate aggregator commissions remain
excluded; physical grid fees and degradation remain separate cost components.

Past auction cash flow and already incurred degradation are sunk at residual
reoptimization. Priced commitments remain in the external ledger, and pending
unpriced submitted quantities enter $Q^{\rm fix}$ without adding an optimizable
cash-flow term. Their future clearing value is constant with respect to all
remaining decisions and is excluded from $J_k$. This avoids double counting.
The initial pre-DA solve has no prior commitments; its objective spans the
modeled whole-day opportunity set. Residual objective values therefore should
not be summed across auction solves to compute daily profit.

There is no explicit curtailment penalty, risk measure, capital cost, fixed O\&M,
tax, brokerage fee, ramp constraint, minimum dwell time, or calendar-aging charge
in~\eqref{eq:objective}. Curtailment can affect revenue through delivery choices,
but is not independently priced. The horizon can exploit inventory when its
terminal target is free; this matters when comparing asset value.

At each decision, remaining-horizon charge/discharge modes, curtailment, grid
powers and energy states are free subject to their balances, boundary state,
nonanticipativity, product rules and terminal condition. Executed past intervals
are absent from the residual grid. Future-auction quantities are plans until

solve. Before noon, IDA3 cannot change orders for the morning; physical control
commands from the previous plan also remain in force. All IDA3 decision
variables cover only the 48 quarter-hours from 12:00 to midnight: charge,
discharge, mode, curtailment, grid power and trades, with 49 SOC boundaries.
Gate closure determines the information cutoff, not the delivery horizon.

More generally, let $c_k$ be the submission cutoff and $j_k$ the first grid
boundary no earlier than both $c_k$ and the current auction's first eligible
delivery. The solver starts at $j_k$. From the latest observed physical state,
the replay projects the locked prefix using the previously requested commands
$\bar u_i$, fixed contracted grid targets $Q_i/\Delta_i$, and the latest
cutoff-available PV forecast $\widehat P^{(c_k)}_i$ through the same physical
controller $\Phi$ used for delivery:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat x_{i+1\mid c_k}
 =\Phi\!\left(\widehat x_{i\mid c_k},\bar u_i,
              Q_i/\Delta_i,\widehat P^{(c_k)}_i\right),\qquad i<j_k.
\end{equation}
Here $x$ includes energy and degradation state. The resulting
$\widehat E_{j_k\mid c_k}$ is the fixed initial energy of the residual MILP.
Forecast publication eligibility is still checked at $c_k$; projecting to noon
does not permit a forecast released after the 10:00 gate. No future measured PV
is used in this projection. Actual delivery continues with the retained commands
and realized PV, so its noon state may differ from the projected state. Grid
feedback and protection enforce the actual SOC and power bounds. If IDA3 is the
first participating auction, zero-position feedback continues until noon.

\section{Battery degradation models}

\subsection{Current AC throughput approximation}
For one trajectory,
\begin{equation}
 H=\sum_t(c_t+d_t)\Delta_t,\qquad D_{\rm thr}=c_{\rm thr}H.
\end{equation}
Both directional AC throughputs are charged once. The YAML can directly supply
$c_{\rm thr}$ or derive it from replacement economics and reference cycle life.
Let $p_{\rm USD}$ be pack USD/kWh, $x$ be USD per EUR, $p_{\rm extra}$ additional
EUR/kWh replacement cost, $s_R$ salvage fraction, $N$ reference cycle life and
$d_{\rm ref}$ reference internal depth of discharge. Then
\begin{align}
 R&=1000E\left(\frac{p_{\rm USD}}{x}+p_{\rm extra}\right)(1-s_R),\label{eq:replacement}\\
 H_{\rm life}&=N d_{\rm ref}E\left(\frac{1}{\eta_c}+\eta_d\right),\\
 c_{\rm thr}&=\frac{R}{H_{\rm life}}.\label{eq:cthr}
\end{align}
One reference internal cycle charges $d_{\rm ref}E/\eta_c$ AC MWh and discharges
$d_{\rm ref}E\eta_d$ AC MWh, explaining the normalization. There is no additional
factor of two or half applied to~\eqref{eq:cthr}. The configured end-of-life
capacity fraction is descriptive metadata, not an extra multiplier or a dynamic
capacity update.

The current inherited farm-910 YAML uses a historical generic pack-price proxy,
a dated FX conversion, and an illustrative reference cycle life. Those are study
inputs with source metadata, not an estimate of a specific installed BESS cost.
The resolved $R$ and $c_{\rm thr}$ are saved. The cost is financial wear; it does
not reduce $E$, $\overline P$, or efficiencies as the simulation proceeds.

\subsection{Optional segmented depth-dependent aging}
The optional \texttt{xu\_segmented} mode follows the configured segmented
cycle-depth construction described in the repository's Xu notes and the author
paper~\cite{xu}. It is distinct from the current throughput study.
For a configurable convex stress curve $\Phi(d)=\alpha d^\beta$, $\beta\ge1$,
partition nominal internal capacity into $J$ equal segments of size $E/J$.
The marginal AC discharge cost of segment $j=1,\ldots,J$ is
\begin{equation}
 m_j=\frac{RJ}{\eta_d E}
       \left[\Phi(j/J)-\Phi((j-1)/J)\right].
\end{equation}
It is nondecreasing with depth. The degradation charge is
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm Xu}=\sum_t\sum_{j=1}^J m_j d_{j,t}\Delta_t.
\end{equation}
Only discharging is charged under this convention. A discharging half-cycle
incurs the convention's depth cost, while a charging half-cycle does not; do not
add an extra factor of one half. Nonzero throughput cost is rejected in this mode.

Segment flows and energies obey
\begin{align}
 \sum_j c_{j,t}&=c_t,& \sum_j d_{j,t}&=d_t,&\sum_j h_{j,t}&=e_t,\\
 h_{j,t+1}&=h_{j,t}+\Delta_t(\eta_c c_{j,t}-d_{j,t}/\eta_d),&
 0\le h_{j,t}&\le E/J.
\end{align}
Segment energies represent cycle-depth memory, not fixed absolute SOC buckets.
Charging and discharging both use shallow available segments first. For each
$j<J$, the implementation adds priorities $v^c_{j,t},v^d_{j,t}\in\{0,1\}$:
\begin{align}
 \sum_{\ell>j}c_{\ell,t}&\le\overline P v^c_{j,t},&
 h_{j,t+1}&\ge(E/J)v^c_{j,t},\\
 \sum_{\ell>j}d_{\ell,t}&\le\overline P v^d_{j,t},&
 h_{j,t+1}&\le(E/J)(1-v^d_{j,t}).
\end{align}
Using a deeper segment therefore requires the shallower segment to end full
when charging or empty when discharging. This chooses a deterministic shallow-
first representative, including otherwise indifferent charging allocations.
Segment actions have the same information ownership as aggregate battery power.

At a declared fresh study start, initialize
\begin{equation}
 h_{j,0}=\clip\big(e_0-(j-1)E/J,\ 0,\ E/J\big).
\end{equation}
Afterwards the segment vector must be carried with actual execution; an arbitrary
residual solve cannot reconstruct it from aggregate SOC. Previous throughput or
aging requires explicit memory. Xu mode currently rejects nonzero self-discharge.
It remains a finite-segment approximation and omits calendar, temperature and
current-rate aging, capacity-fade feedback and unknown pre-study cycle history.

\section{Submission, publication and reoptimization workflow}

Before each active auction submission, the GME replay refreshes the latest
available PV forecast over unexecuted intervals, projects a boundary if necessary,
and solves the residual planning problem. Previously submitted quantities,
including still-unpriced ones, are fixed. At submission, only the current
auction's node-shared action is extracted. Later planned auction quantities are
not booked and can change.

At publication, known prices are updated, and actual contractual revenue
$\pi^{\rm real}_{a,t}q^{\rm sub}_{a,t}$ is entered exactly once in the ledger.
Publication does not reopen a market. The current GME experiment forces
\texttt{reoptimize\_physical\_controls=false}; it uses four auction solves for
four active auctions, not an extra set of publication solves or 96 physical
optimizations. Physical feedback is algebraic. Synthetic replay can separately
enable interval-control reoptimization, so its solve count need not be four.

At the next auction, closed commitments are carried, remaining open opportunities
are reconsidered, and past executed state/aging remain fixed. In the actual-price
oracle all future auction prices are already supplied as a benchmark, so price
publication does not remove price uncertainty there. In a forecast-based route,
known results condition subsequent price distributions without changing the
commitments that produced them.

\section{Physical feedback with realized PV}

\subsection{Feasible interval power range}
The current policy is \texttt{track\_committed\_grid}. For interval $t$ its target is
\begin{equation}
 g_t^\star=Q_t^{\rm committed}/\Delta_t.
\end{equation}
The controller uses effective realized interval-average PV $\widetilde V_t$;
this is an idealized interval simulator, not a subsecond telemetry/control model.
It does not disclose this final average to the earlier auction optimizer.
Given actual initial energy $e_t$ and retained energy $\bar e_t=\rho_t e_t$,
the feasible charging/discharging maxima are
\begin{align}
 C_t&=\min\left\{\overline P,
           \frac{\pos{E^{\max}-\bar e_t}}{\eta_c\Delta_t}\right\},\\
 D_t&=\min\left\{\overline P,
           \frac{\eta_d\pos{\bar e_t-E^{\min}}}{\Delta_t}\right\}.
\end{align}
First clip the planned charge/discharge commands to these maxima, obtaining
$c_t^0,d_t^0$. Simultaneous positive requested charging and discharging after
protection is rejected. Initially curtail only available PV:
$u_t^0=\min\{\widetilde V_t,\pos{u_t^{\rm plan}}\}$.

\subsection{Grid-tracking policy}
Release scheduled curtailment if it can help meet a deficit:
\begin{align}
 g_t^0&=\widetilde V_t-u_t^0+d_t^0-c_t^0,\\
 \bar u_t&=u_t^0-\min\{u_t^0,\pos{g_t^\star-g_t^0}\}.
\end{align}
The desired net battery dispatch and its feasible projection are
\begin{align}
 b_t^{\rm req}&=g_t^\star-\widetilde V_t+\bar u_t,\\
 b_t&=\clip(b_t^{\rm req},-C_t,D_t),\\
 c_t&=\pos{-b_t},\qquad d_t=\pos{b_t}.
\end{align}
Finally curtail any remaining surplus that battery adjustment cannot absorb:
\begin{equation}
 u_t=\bar u_t+
 \min\{\widetilde V_t-\bar u_t,
             \pos{\widetilde V_t-\bar u_t+b_t-g_t^\star}\}.
\end{equation}
Thus excess PV first decreases discharge, then increases charging as needed and
feasible, then is curtailed. A PV shortfall first releases planned curtailment,
then reduces charging or increases discharge within the actual SOC box. The
remaining tracking error is preserved if battery power/energy cannot supply it.

POI protection follows. If net import exceeds its rating, charging is reduced.
If export exceeds its rating, discharge is reduced first and remaining excess
PV is curtailed. Grid power is recomputed consistently with the adjusted actions.
Actual energy then advances using~\eqref{eq:soc} with these executed powers, and
bounds are checked before accepting the new state. Both throughput/Xu memory and
cumulative financial wear are advanced from executed actions, not from the old
auction plan. Protection is mandatory under either execution policy; the
alternative \texttt{follow\_battery\_plan} skips grid-tracking adjustment.

There is no controller constraint forcing the actual terminal energy to the
auction plan's target. Forecast errors and grid tracking can cause an ending-SOC
difference even if every optimization imposed~\eqref{eq:terminal}. This is visible
in the current terminal-50\% study and is not equivalent to guaranteeing 50\%
realized SOC. Any hard actual-terminal policy would be an additional controller
design and could create different imbalances/cash flows.

\subsection{PV source allocation for reporting}
For the executed trajectory, use the explicit PV-first accounting convention
\begin{align}
 P^{\rm PV\to B}_t&=\min\{\widetilde V_t-u_t,c_t\},\\
 P^{\rm PV\to G}_t&=\widetilde V_t-u_t-P^{\rm PV\to B}_t,\\
 P^{\rm PV\to curt}_t&=u_t.
\end{align}
They sum to $\widetilde V_t$ in every interval. Grid-supplied charging and battery
discharge are not counted as PV production. These are post-processing flows;
there are no separate source-attribution decision variables in the MILP.

\section{Settlement and expected-versus-realized accounting}

Actual net deviation is retained at the native quarter-hour:
\begin{equation}
 \delta_t^{\rm real}=g_t^{\rm real}\Delta_t-Q_t^{\rm committed}.
\end{equation}
Positive means more net export than promised. With the selected single Terna
NORD settlement price $\lambda_t$, the modeled cash flow is
\begin{equation}
 S_t=\lambda_t\delta_t^{\rm real}.
\end{equation}
A positive price therefore does not mean every imbalance is penalized: positive
excess export is credited and a negative deviation is debited. Negative prices
are allowed and reverse these cash-flow signs. The general settlement helper
also accepts two directional curves:
\begin{equation}
 S_t=\lambda_t^+\pos{\delta_t^{\rm real}}
       -\lambda_t^-\pos{-\delta_t^{\rm real}}.
\end{equation}
This documents the implemented study accounting, not every possible Italian
participant-specific settlement item or regulatory regime. The workbook adapter
selects the declared NORD macrozone and the imbalance-price column, retaining
other imported fields as provenance. Product starts and ends must match the
execution grid exactly. Prices are neither hourly-averaged nor filled across
gaps; missing enabled settlement prices are an error.

Actual daily auction cash flow and settled operating profit are
\begin{align}
 A^{\rm real}&=\sum_{a,t}\pi^{\rm real}_{a,t}q^{\rm sub}_{a,t},\\
 J^{\rm real}&=A^{\rm real}+\sum_t S_t
       -D^{\rm executed}-f\sum_t\pos{-g_t^{\rm real}}\Delta_t-C^{\rm trading,real}.
\end{align}
A model deterrence penalty is not an actual settlement charge and is not
subtracted a second time by actual post-processing. If settlement is unavailable,
profit before settlement is reported and fully settled profit remains unknown.
Only actually submitted guaranteed transactions enter $C^{\rm trading,real}$;
future plans that are never submitted contribute no realized fees. The ledger
retains transaction-month volume, per-trade charges and tariff versions. An
independent Python calculation reconciles those charges and the final monthly
state. Expected and realized transaction fees are reported separately from gross
auction revenue. In \path{realized_profit_breakdown.png}, trading-fee EUR and
gross MWh occupy the former import-fee slot; nonzero physical grid fees appear
as their own additional bar.

Let $J^{\rm init}$ and $A^{\rm init}$ be the initial auction solve's expected
profit and auction revenue. Saved mismatches are
\begin{equation}
 \Delta J=J^{\rm real}-J^{\rm init},\qquad
 \Delta A=A^{\rm real}-A^{\rm init},\qquad
 r_J=100\frac{\Delta J}{|J^{\rm init}|}.
\end{equation}
The percentage is undefined/\texttt{null} when its expectation is zero; unsettled
profit comparisons remain unknown. Differences include later replanning and
physical execution effects, not only price-forecast error.

The replay also evaluates each stored auction snapshot as an open-loop diagnostic
against realized PV and the same settlement prices. Its planned battery commands
are protected physically. Deviations are measured against that snapshot's planned
grid schedule (which can include assumed future trades) or the final contract,
as labeled in the recorded comparisons. It is not another auction
optimization. Because IDA3 covers a shorter residual horizon, a common-horizon
comparison is also saved; unaligned whole-horizon totals should not be compared
as if their delivery intervals were identical.

\section{Value of reoptimization through frozen-plan replay}

The optional frozen-plan comparison asks what would happen if planning stopped
after one auction decision but physical feedback continued. This differs from
the older open-loop imbalance diagnostic, which follows planned battery commands
without grid-tracking feedback and does not reconstruct an alternative complete
auction ledger and net profit.

Let $k$ be an active auction other than the final active auction. At its cutoff,
retain the entire deterministic future-trade vector from that solve. Define
\begin{equation}
 q^{[k]}_{a,t}=
 \begin{cases}
 q^{\rm submitted,ref}_{a,t},&a\le k,\\
 q^{\mathrm{plan},k}_{a,t},&a>k,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad Q^{[k]}_t=\sum_a q^{[k]}_{a,t}.
\end{equation}
The already submitted trades are fixed even if their prices are unpublished.
Future orders are the frozen snapshot's orders, not the later realized orders
from the reoptimized strategy. They are submitted at their original gates and
priced at actual clearing prices. Their eligibility must ensure submission
precedes the relevant delivery interval. Thus their cumulative position is a
valid delivery-time tracking target without prematurely revealing new prices or
forecasts to the frozen plan.

Executed history before the frozen residual boundary remains identical to the
reference trajectory. The suffix starts from the actual carried energy,
throughput, incurred wear and, for Xu mode, segment-memory vector. It is not
reset to the snapshot's forecast SOC, including when the cutoff projects a
not-yet-observed boundary. At physical delivery that boundary's actual state is
carried forward without changing the frozen decisions.
The same configured execution policy uses the frozen battery/curtailment
commands, target $Q^{[k]}_t/\Delta_t$, and the same effective realized PV:
\begin{equation}
 (c_t^{[k]},d_t^{[k]},u_t^{[k]},e_{t+1}^{[k]})
 =\mathcal F(e_t^{[k]},\widetilde V_t,Q_t^{[k]}/\Delta_t,
             c_t^{\rm plan,k},d_t^{\rm plan,k},u_t^{\rm plan,k}).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal F$ is the grid-feedback/protection policy described above. The
current study uses grid tracking; the alternative execution policy, if selected,
is shared with the reference. No later optimization or weather-forecast update
is applied. The algebraic replay adds zero optimization solves.

Using the same signed settlement convention and actual dated transaction-fee
accounting as the reference, the full-day frozen profit is
\begin{equation}
 J_{\rm frozen}^{[k]}=
 \sum_{a,t}\pi_{a,t}^{\rm real}q_{a,t}^{[k]}
 +\sum_t S_t^{[k]}-D^{[k]}-C_{\rm import}^{[k]}
 -C_{\rm trading}^{[k]}.
\end{equation}
Previously executed prefix costs are preserved. Future wear is recomputed from
the counterfactual's executed commands; gross fees are recomputed from its own
trade ledger and the same initial operator-volume state, including month
transitions and tariff bands. Reference future trades or fees are never borrowed
after the freeze point.

The value KPI is
\begin{equation}
 G_k=J_{\rm fully\ reoptimized}^{\rm real}-J_{\rm frozen}^{[k]}.
\end{equation}
Both sides cover the entire delivery day, so differing residual lengths do not
create incomparable profit totals. Positive values favor later reoptimization;
negative values are retained. Each $G_k$ measures all later replanning after
$k$: the values are not additive and need not be monotonic. This comparison
includes new forecasts, revised orders and state-aware planning rather than
isolating a causal effect of forecast accuracy. Final stored-energy differences
are reported; no terminal salvage price is assumed. If actual settlement is
unavailable, settled profit and $G_k$ remain unknown.

The independent YAML section is \path{reoptimization_baseline}.
Its options are \texttt{enabled}, \path{freeze_after_auctions}, and \texttt{plots}.
An absent section disables the feature; the comparison study enables it.
A null auction selection evaluates every active pre-final auction, while a
list chooses a subset. The last active auction is always excluded, cancelled
auctions produce no baseline, and an empty selection produces no comparisons.
The plot switch also respects the global plot setting. Snapshot replay requires
deterministic plans; it rejects using one selected leaf of a contingent plan as
if all of its future branches were already known.

The simulator is \path{src/simulation/frozen_plans.py}. Saved dispatch-plan rows
include every \texttt{planned\_trade\_<auction>\_mwh} and their planned net position.
Full-day profits, gains and states are saved in the frozen-plan summary CSV/JSON,
with separate actual counterfactual dispatch and trade ledgers. Individual gains
also enter the scalar KPI table, and \texttt{reoptimization\_value.png} compares
frozen profits with the reference. The isolated January configuration is
\path{configs/full_experiments/gme_oracle_2025-01-01_farm910_reoptimization_value.yaml}.

\section{Perfect-information schedules and the PV-only baseline}

\subsection{PV+BESS versus the feedback controller}
After replay, one additional full-day problem uses $V_t=\widetilde V_t$ and all
actual auction-price curves, with a single scenario and all auction orders
jointly free subject to their native products, eligibility, POI and physical
constraints. It retains the BESS ratings, efficiencies, SOC box, degradation,
initial energy and configured terminal target. Call its achieved solver
objective $J_{\rm PV+BESS}^{\rm PI}$.

This is deliberately a hindsight plan rather than a causally executable policy.
It is compared with the feedback controller's actual dispatch, curtailment and
SOC, and its objective is saved as the solver's \texttt{ObjVal}. Four missing
measurement intervals, if permitted by the selected run, are handled through
that run's explicit fallback; diagnostics record their count and identities.
The schedule figure shows both trajectories and their battery-power difference
through the final day boundary. It does not assert that feedback should reproduce
the hindsight trajectory or terminal energy.

\subsection{PV-only asset counterfactual}
When \texttt{experiment\_outputs.pv\_only\_baseline: true}, a second independent
solve uses the same optimizer builder with
\begin{equation}
 E=0,\quad\overline P=0,\quad E^{\min}=E^{\max}=0,\quad e_0=e_T=0,
 \quad D=0.
\end{equation}
Battery powers and energy variables are consequently fixed to zero; the code
reuses one physical/trading formulation instead of implementing a second,
potentially inconsistent PV-only model. Unused variables can be removed by
solver presolve. PV injection/curtailment and auction trades remain free subject
to the same effective realized PV, native products, active auctions and POI.
Call its achieved objective $J_{\rm PV}^{\rm PI}$.

The incremental operating-value KPI is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:value}
 \Delta J_{\rm BESS}^{\rm PI}
       =J_{\rm PV+BESS}^{\rm PI}-J_{\rm PV}^{\rm PI}.
\end{equation}
The BESS side includes its own modeled degradation; the PV-only side has no
battery wear. Both sides retain permitted cross-auction spread trading, so
market-trading revenue common to both is not credited to battery ownership.
The KPI does not subtract realized feedback wear from a different ideal schedule.
Each counterfactual reports its own objective components. Directly subtracting
a PV-only hindsight objective from the realized PV+BESS feedback profit would
mix information and execution policies and is not the asset-value KPI.

This comparison is one-day operating value before capital expenditure, fixed
operating costs, financing and taxes. A free terminal target can also use initial
stored energy or leave different inventory; the selected 50\% initial/terminal
hindsight comparison avoids that particular inventory difference for the BESS
model. Realized feedback may still finish at a different SOC. No residual-energy
valuation, annualization, payback, NPV or investment optimization is implemented.
Those need a representative horizon and additional cost/cash-flow assumptions.

Setting the switch to \texttt{false} skips the PV-only solve and its model, JSON,
CSV and figure. Incremental-value metrics become \texttt{null} and an explicit
baseline-enabled KPI is false. The perfect-information PV+BESS-versus-feedback
comparison still runs. Four active auctions therefore produce four replay
solves plus one PV+BESS hindsight solve and, when enabled, one PV-only solve.
Counterfactual solve diagnostics are separate from replay solve counts.

\section{Configurable participation and matched-day profile comparison}

Participation is a choice of a nonempty subset $P$ of the chronologically ordered
auction set $\mathcal A=\{\mathrm{DA},\mathrm{IDA1},\mathrm{IDA2},\mathrm{IDA3}\}$.
The single-study configuration is
\path{gme_oracle_experiment.participating_auctions}. Omission or null selects every
configured auction dataset. Gate order remains unchanged; unselected auctions
have no price-data requirement, trade variables, submission event/solve or fees.
Their orders are absent rather than independent variables fixed after a solve.
The active set on day $d$ also removes documented/configured cancellations.
If every selected auction is documented/configured as cancelled, the profile
retains its full delivery day with zero orders, auction/control solves and
auction fees. The zero-position feedback policy stores PV within the SOC box
and curtails remaining surplus; executed wear and settlement remain in actual
profit. There is no initial auction objective (its expectation KPIs are null),
and no auction plan enforces the configured terminal target on that day. Missing
or partial price data without cancellation evidence still fail.

Four independently optional auctions produce
\begin{equation}
 |\{P\subseteq\mathcal A:P\ne\varnothing\}|=2^4-1=15.
\end{equation}
If DA is mandatory and the three IDA auctions remain optional, there are instead
\begin{equation}
 |\{\{\mathrm{DA}\}\cup S:S\subseteq\{\mathrm{IDA1},\mathrm{IDA2},\mathrm{IDA3}\}\}|=2^3=8.
\end{equation}
Both families and explicitly listed profiles are supported. For example,
$P=\{\mathrm{IDA1},\mathrm{IDA2}\}$ and $P=\{\mathrm{IDA3}\}$ are valid without DA.
All physical, native-product, degradation and settlement constraints continue to
apply to the same full delivery day.

If the first selected gate occurs after delivery begins, its prefix uses zero
contracted grid delivery and grid feedback with zero planned battery commands.
The controller may store PV within the energy box and curtail remaining surplus;
it does not earn uncontracted export revenue in this prefix. Actual energy and
wear carry to the first selected solve, including the existing unfinished-interval
state projection. No extra auction optimization is inserted before the first
selected decision. Prefix executed/projected costs are subtracted from the first
residual objective when forming an initial whole-day expected profit; the residual
model objective is separately retained. Actual whole-day profit already includes
all executed prefix costs through the ordinary financial accounting.

\subsection{Sequential current-auction-only baseline}

At the decision for auction $a$, integrated planning includes the open auctions
$\mathcal A_a$. The configurable sequential baseline replaces this set by
$\{a\}$. Let $Q_t^{<a}$ denote all previously submitted, fixed quantities,
including unpriced commitments. Its only free market quantity is $q_{a,t}$:
\begin{equation}
 Q_t=Q_t^{<a}+q_{a,t},\qquad
 \max\ \sum_t \lambda_{a,t}q_{a,t}
       -C_{\mathrm{deg}}-C_{\mathrm{import}}-C_{\mathrm{fees}}.
\end{equation}
The existing physical balances link $Q_t$ to grid delivery. The full remaining
delivery horizon retains charge/discharge, curtailment, SOC transitions, native
product equalities, POI limits and the configured terminal target. No later
auction trade or revenue appears. Sunk earlier cash flow does not affect the
choice. Each next gate receives updated PV, actual carried battery state and
immutable prior submissions. Under the price-oracle convention, the current
auction's actual clearing curve is supplied to that solve.

\path{gme_oracle_experiment.auction_planning_mode} selects
\texttt{integrated} (default) or \texttt{current\_auction\_only}.
\path{participation_study.sequential_all_auctions_baseline} optionally adds
one all-auction sequential result beside the integrated participation profiles.
The January study refresh comprises 465 integrated cases and 31 sequential
cases. Matched daily realized profits, their paired differences, model sizes,
forecast inputs and fallback decisions are saved. A later infeasible sequential
solve retains prior physical commands and submits zero new trades; it has no
previously planned future order to submit.

\subsection{Operational fallback after an infeasible auction decision}

Exact planned delivery does not guarantee feasible recourse after forecast
updates. Limited remaining energy, power and trading capacity may conflict with
the configured terminal state or remaining delivery obligations. The physical
controller can continue within SOC/power limits by accepting an imbalance even
when the planning model is infeasible. A pre-noon delivery shortfall is executed
and settled under the retained policy; those closed intervals are excluded from
the IDA3 MILP and cannot make it infeasible.

The configurable policy \path{execution.infeasible_auction_policy} defaults to
\texttt{error}. The full-January study uses \texttt{previous\_plan}. When and only
when the solver confirms infeasibility, this policy uses the last feasible
deterministic snapshot for the current auction's previously unsubmitted orders
and remaining requested physical commands:
\begin{equation}
 q^{\rm submit}_{a,t}=q^{\rm last\ feasible}_{a,t},\qquad
 u^{\rm requested}_t=u^{\rm last\ feasible}_t.
\end{equation}
The orders are submitted at their actual gate and priced at actual clearing
prices with ordinary fee accounting; existing commitments remain fixed. The
same feedback controller executes against actual PV, respects the physical
limits, and settles unavoidable deviations ex post. This is a retained plan,
not a new feasible optimum under the updated forecast. No recovery optimization
or constraint relaxation is introduced. Subsequent selected decisions still
attempt their normal reoptimization. Missing prior plans, nondeterministic
snapshots and other solver failures remain errors.

Failed decision/forecast diagnostics, reused plan sources, submitted quantities
and attempt counts are saved separately from successful solves; optional IIS
reports use \path{execution.save_infeasibility_diagnostics}. Summary tables retain
fallback-day and failed-decision counts rather than omitting those dates from
the comparison. The physical trajectory can finish away from the terminal SOC
target of its retained plan, just as under ordinary forecast-error feedback.

The \texttt{experiment-participation} command runs configured profiles on a common
set of delivery days $\mathcal D$. The ranking metric is
\begin{equation}
 \overline J(P)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal D|}
       \sum_{d\in\mathcal D}J^{\rm real}(P,d),\qquad
 P^\star_{\mathcal D}\in\arg\max_P\overline J(P).
\end{equation}
It is an empirical ranking on $\mathcal D$, not an established optimum for unseen
days. Selecting a different winning profile after each observed day would evaluate
$|\mathcal D|^{-1}\sum_d\max_P J^{\rm real}(P,d)$ and would use additional hindsight;
the saved daily-winner table is diagnostic and is not the fixed-profile ranking.

Every profile must complete exactly the same requested dates with fully priced
actual settlement. Missing or invalid inputs fail the batch; they do not silently
change a profile's averaging sample. Battery and operator fee initial state are
identical across profiles and reset independently each study day. This is not a
continuous multi-day carry of SOC or monthly traded volume. The initialization
weather cutoff is common before the earliest candidate gate and delivery start.
Later forecast updates occur only at selected auction decisions. Delivery grids,
initial PV forecasts, effective realized PV (including configured fallback
intervals) and shared actual auction-price curves are fingerprinted and checked
for equality across profiles. The market adapter resolves native auction-product
durations for each date. Actual price snapshots, model inputs and all daily
decisions remain in the per-profile run artifacts.

Summary outputs retain average, total, minimum, maximum and median daily profit,
10th/90th percentiles, daily population standard deviation, negative-profit and
zero-active-auction day counts, daily wins including configured tolerance ties,
paired differences to the largest selected profile, mean SOC/energy, curtailment,
wear, transaction fees and active auction/solve counts. Final stored energy is
reported without salvage valuation. The current configured terminal SOC remains
a planning constraint rather than a guarantee on feedback's final state, so cash
profit and ending inventory must be assessed together. Fee and inventory reset
assumptions also matter when extrapolating to a longer investment horizon.

The comparison figure places a separate gold perfect-foresight reference above
the ranked profiles. Its mean and daily standard deviation use the full-day
perfect-information objective values (Gurobi \texttt{ObjVal}) on the identical
dates, effective realized PV, assets, costs and configured terminal SOC, with
every configured auction that remains available. This reference includes markets
outside a restricted candidate/profile family. Existing all-auction benchmark
outputs are reused; if that profile is absent, one additional perfect-information
solve per day supplies the reference without another auction replay. Its daily
objectives and summary are retained separately, along with objective-minus-profit
differences in the profile tables. This is a model-objective reference rather than
an additional feedback participation rank: actual imbalance settlements and
realized terminal inventory can differ between the accounting cases.
\path{participation_study.perfect_foresight_reference} enables this feature by
default and can be set to false.

The batch YAML section \path{participation_study} controls candidate/required
auctions, explicit profile lists, common dates, output paths, per-profile
figures/models, summary plotting and profit-tie tolerance.
The supplied example is
\path{configs/full_experiments/gme_participation_2025-01-01_03.yaml}.
The full-month independent-day configuration is
\path{configs/full_experiments/gme_participation_2025-01.yaml}, with 31 common
delivery days and 465 profile/day cases. Fully cancelled IDA3-only cases remain
in the averaging sample rather than changing that profile's denominator.
The driver is \path{src/simulation/participation_study.py}, while
\path{src/participation.py} validates/enumerates subsets. Summary CSV/JSON/plots
are stored apart from each individual replay under its profile/day directory.
Optional \path{participation_study.resume} verifies the same effective
configuration, completed child manifests, input fingerprints and saved profits
before reusing checkpoints. Incomplete children are retained separately and
rerun; changed configurations are rejected. After independent solution
validation, tolerated negative floating-point command roundoff is floored to
zero before the physical controller's strict nonnegative-input checks.

\section{Continuous operation with daily optimization horizons}

\subsection{Delivery books and the chronological event sequence}
The separate \texttt{experiment-continuous} workflow selects inclusive local
delivery dates $d\in\mathcal D=\{d_0,\ldots,d_F\}$ through
\path{continuous_optimization.start_date} and \path{end_date}. Each day has its
own physical grid $\mathcal T_d$, native auction products, actual price curves
and auction calendar. Commitments and revisable plans are keyed by delivery
day as well as auction and interval, for example $(d,a,t)$; the same auction
name on another delivery day is a different market book. This is continuous
chronological execution with daily planning horizons; it is not a single
multi-day MILP, and a later-day solve cannot revise an earlier delivery book.

All events are merged into one chronological sequence. On an ordinary operating
day $d$, the replay submits IDA3 orders for $d$, then DA, IDA1 and IDA2 orders
for $d+1$ while executing delivery $d$. Submissions use the configured decision
buffer before the local gate; price publications book already submitted orders
without triggering another optimization. IDA3 variables remain restricted to
noon--midnight of their delivery day. Native DA-hour equalities and quarter-hour
IDA/physical controls are unchanged. Cancelled auctions produce no order,
variables or solve, and unexplained missing inputs fail preflight.

This is a chronological sequence of \emph{daily} MILPs, not a simultaneous
month-long or two-day trading MILP. An optimization for $d+1$ cannot change
current-day dispatch or commitments for $d$. Conversely, current-day IDA3
cannot reopen submitted next-day DA quantities. The current-day commands and
book enter next-day planning through the projected boundary state below.

\subsection{Forecast-based projection of the next initial state}
At a decision cutoff $c_k$, let $x_k^{\rm obs}$ be the most recent observed
physical state at boundary $\tau_{i_k}\le c_k$. This state includes energy,
cumulative executed throughput/degradation and, when relevant, depth-model
segment history. The current continuous controller requires zero configured
telemetry latency, treating an interval's actual state as observable at its
end; preflight rejects a nonzero \path{source.telemetry_latency_minutes}.
This restriction is independent of the weather forecast's availability lag.
An unfinished interval's already issued command is retained.
Let $\bar u_i$ be the retained current-day charge, discharge and PV-curtailment
commands and $Q_i^{\rm sub}$ the cumulative submitted grid position. For a
pre-delivery DA, IDA1 or IDA2 solve for $d$, the planning boundary $b(d,a)$ is
midnight at the start of $d$; for IDA3 it is noon on $d$. Define a shadow state
using only the weather/PV vintage available at $c_k$:
\begin{align}
 \widehat x_{i_k\mid k}&=x_k^{\rm obs},\\
 \widehat x_{i+1\mid k}
 &=\Phi\!\left(\widehat x_{i\mid k},\bar u_i,
        Q_i^{\rm sub}/\Delta_i,\widehat P_{i\mid c_k}\right),
       &&i_k\le i<b(d,a). \label{eq:continuous-shadow}
\end{align}
$\Phi$ is the same grid-tracking and physical-protection controller used for
actual execution. Thus the forecast projection respects SOC, battery power and
POI limits while preserving the old commands and submitted grid target. It
uses no new optimization for the locked prefix and no future measured PV.
Forecast eligibility uses the UTC publication/availability clock at $c_k$,
including the configured six-hour lag, independently of the later projected
delivery boundary.

The daily MILP fixes its initial state to
$x_{d,b}=\widehat x_{b(d,a)\mid k}$. At a later gate this projection is recomputed
from the newer actual state and latest eligible forecast. Meanwhile actual
execution advances separately:
\begin{equation}
 x^{\rm real}_{i+1}=\Phi\!\left(x^{\rm real}_i,\bar u_i,
       Q_i^{\rm sub}/\Delta_i,P_i^{\rm real}\right).
\end{equation}
Neither solving nor revising a shadow projection overwrites actual energy or
aging state. The realized midnight state may differ from the previously
projected state; later solves carry the actual state as it becomes available.
Saved boundary records retain prediction, actual state and signed error
$E_b^{\rm real}-\widehat E_{b\mid k}$. Midnight projections are distinguished
from IDA3 noon projections in diagnostics and plots.

\subsection{Daily free decisions, initial condition and final target}
For integrated planning, the free trade quantities are those of the current
and remaining active auctions \emph{for that delivery day}. For sequential
\texttt{current\_auction\_only} planning, only the current auction has free
trade variables and expected trading revenue. Previously submitted quantities
for the target book, including unpublished pending orders, remain fixed in
$Q^{\rm fix}_{d,t}$ in both modes. Their cash values remain external to the
new decision objective. Physical controls and SOC over the target day's
remaining eligible delivery horizon remain free under the existing balances,
costs and asset constraints. Trades in other delivery books are not decision
variables in this MILP. Its objective is the daily residual
objective~\eqref{eq:objective}; projected costs already locked before its
planning boundary are not new trading opportunities.

For the supplied January configuration, $d_0=$ 1 January 2025 and $d_F=$
31 January 2025. The supplied boundary is
\begin{equation}
 E^{\rm real}_{d_0,0}=0.90\,E^{\rm nominal}.
\end{equation}
Trading starts with the 31 December DA/IDA1/IDA2 events for delivery on
1 January. Their initial midnight state is this supplied assumption; it is
not inferred from unavailable 31 December physical operation. Eligible
31 December forecasts and the actual 1 January delivery-price curves are
still required. After the first midnight, there is no daily SOC reset.

The configured terminal constraint is applied only when planning the final
delivery day and only when the target is non-null:
\begin{equation}
 e_{d_F,|\mathcal T_{d_F}|}
   =E^{\rm nominal}\,s_F/100,\qquad s_F=50
       \quad\hbox{(SOC percentage in the January example)}.
\end{equation}
Intermediate midnight states have no equality target or energy salvage price.
They remain inside their SOC box and, when configured, satisfy the additional
planned minimum in~\eqref{eq:terminal-minimum}. The January 2025 template
leaves that minimum disabled; the paired January 2026 studies use 40\%. Actual terminal energy remains
an output of feedback execution and need not equal the planned target. The
last delivery interval ends at local midnight on 1 February. No orders for
1 February delivery are submitted by the January study.

\subsection{Independent trajectories, accounting and restart}
The default experiment executes three independent chronologies: integrated
planning, sequential current-auction-only planning and an integrated
perfect-PV reference with the same daily horizons. All start from the same
supplied state and use matched actual price, settlement, effective measured
PV and asset inputs, but each carries its own decisions and realized state.
The reference replaces forecast PV by effective realized PV, including in
shadow projections. It does not introduce future delivery-day trade variables.
Its reported performance is continuous settled operating profit, not the sum
of overlapping auction objective values. The two trading modes and this
reference are independently configurable.

The reference defaults to one optimization per delivery day, at DA (or the
first active auction if DA is cancelled). The flag
\path{continuous_optimization.perfect_pv_reoptimize_at_each_auction} defaults
to \texttt{false}; setting it to \texttt{true} restores optimization at every
active gate. With the default, all later auction quantities are read from
the initial integrated plan and submitted only at their own gates, while its
physical commands remain in force throughout delivery. Publication, fees,
actual state carryover and settlement follow the same chronological rules.
The event trace identifies each retained submission separately from solver
attempts. Perfect information preserves the initial plan's attainable value;
repeated solves can select different schedules when several optima exist,
and finite MIP tolerance can permit small objective improvements.

Monthly operating profit is additive over delivery products:
\begin{equation}
 J_{\mathcal D}^{\rm real}=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}\left[
   \sum_{a,t}\pi^{\rm real}_{d,a,t}q^{\rm sub}_{d,a,t}
   +\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_d}S_t
   -D_d^{\rm executed}-C_d^{\rm import}-C_d^{\rm trading,real}\right].
\end{equation}
The trade ledger preserves submission/publication clocks; daily profit is
attributed to delivery date. Fees and auction revenues reconcile both by day
and over the complete ledger. Operator fee volume still uses the submission
month: the first day's 31 December orders belong to December, even though
their revenue appears in 1 January delivery profit. Actual fee mode therefore
requires an applicable December tariff and a timestamped initial operator
volume state; January volume starts according to the fee calendar, without a
daily reset. Wear is charged only on actual executed throughput/history.
Per-day initial expectations and realized-minus-expected differences are
reported separately; no objective values across overlapping gate solves are
added to obtain profit.

With the configured infeasibility policy, a later failed solve can retain the
previous physical plan and its already planned current order in integrated
mode; the sequential mode retains dispatch and submits zero new trades. No
prior plan means the existing failure behavior applies. Actual feedback still
enforces physical limits and settles remaining deviations. Failed attempts
and fallback choices remain explicit diagnostics.

Resume checks the effective configuration, code/policy and common input
fingerprints before restoring an atomic completed-midnight checkpoint. The
checkpoint contains actual physical/aging state, outstanding priced and
unpriced commitments for both relevant books, retained commands, fee state,
publication status and the event cursor. Work after that boundary is replayed
without double-booking cash or fees. Business decisions rolled back for resume
do not survive as submitted history; every actual Gurobi attempt, including
discarded work, survives in a separate attempt log with its rollback marker.
Native \texttt{Model.Runtime} statistics include these attempts, keep reference
solves identifiable, and report totals plus mean/minimum/maximum/population
standard deviation by strategy and delivery day. Model-build and Python wall
times remain separate. Resolved configurations, common-input provenance,
daily/monthly KPIs, state projections and comparisons are saved by file type
under \path{outputs/continuous_optimization/<run_id>/}.

\subsection{Weather-only model search and causal forecast-error adaptation}
The subsequent farm 901/910 workflow removes proxy coordinates, derived solar
and tracker geometry, physical PV intermediates, auction embeddings, month
embeddings and elapsed-age inputs. Its regressors are forecast weather,
cyclic hour/year/month, cycle hour, lead and train-only missingness indicators.
Separate per-farm models predict AC fractions. Nameplate power scales and
clips the output; it is not an estimated weather feature. Measured AC targets
remain unchanged and satellite GHI never enters forecasting features.

The original chronology remains: fit to 2025, select parameters and stopping
epochs on January--April 2026, and report May--August 2026. The latter months
have been inspected in successive research iterations and are therefore a
reused holdout. Fitting and selection code does not use their scores, but they
do not constitute an untouched test of the research process. The existing
availability embargo, six-hour weather lag, buffered cutoffs, native quarters,
DST and IDA3 noon eligibility are preserved. All valid training quarters,
including genuine zeros, are retained. Reported comparisons use the same
frozen daylight mask and equal day/auction weights as the earlier benchmark.

Let $r_f(X)$ be a Ridge weather model fitted only to the 2025 training rows.
New statistical candidates use histogram gradient boosting, Extra Trees,
Huber regression, spline expansions with Ridge, and random Fourier features
with Ridge. Boosting, spline and Fourier candidates can predict directly or
correct $r_f$. Neural candidates include residual MLPs, mixtures of experts,
bidirectional GRUs, Transformers and direct/residual temporal convolutions.
A residual candidate has the form
\begin{equation}
 \widehat P_{dat}=P_{\rm AC,rated}D_{dt}
 \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\{r_f(X_{dat})+g_\theta(X_{da,\cdot})_t\}.
\end{equation}
Direct TCNs instead apply a sigmoid output head. Every sequence uses only
weather already available at issuance. Training uses AdamW, configured
MSE/Huber loss, gradient clipping, deterministic seeds and validation-only
early stopping. Exported convolution artifacts preserve the training padding
length, including 25-hour DST days, to reproduce individual-day predictions.

Two searches complete 176 trial fits: 128 broad statistical/neural trials and
48 additional direct/residual TCN trials across the two farms. At most 42
single-thread CPU workers run concurrently. Validation selects equal-weight
means of its best two, three or five new models. A further 96 comparisons
test causal scalar correction, with four history windows and two minimum-day
requirements over six base predictors per farm. For issuance cutoff $c$,
write $H(c,W)$ for completed days observed by $c$ within the trailing $W$ days.
With $\bar p_{dt}$ the mean base prediction over overlapping auction views,
the gain is
\begin{equation}
 k_c=\operatorname{clip}_{[k_{\min},k_{\max}]}
 \frac{\sum_{d\in H(c,W),t}\bar p_{dt}P_{dt}^{\rm measured}}
      {\sum_{d\in H(c,W),t}\bar p_{dt}^{\,2}},\qquad
 \widehat P^{\rm adapt}_{dat}=
 \operatorname{clip}_{[0,P_{\rm AC,rated}]}(k_c\widehat P_{dat}).
\end{equation}
The gain is one until sufficient completed history and positive denominator
are available. Each physical target is counted once. A day becomes available
only after its next local midnight and all retained targets are observed.
This adapts forecast errors without asserting an aging mechanism. It adds
past 2026 measurements to a predictor whose static weights remain fitted to
2025, and is labelled separately from frozen-model comparisons.

\subsection{Local-hour correction of the existing selected predictors}
The separate \texttt{study-pv-hourly-bias} command corrects the previous
selected farm 901/910 predictors without changing their fitted weights. For

averaged and measured AC is counted once. Let $H_h(c,W)$ contain quarters in

\begin{equation}
 b_h(c)=\frac{\sum_{(d,t)\in H_h(c,W)}

The historical study begins its correction history on 1 January 2026,

\subsection{CSV-authoritative cleaned PV model search}

The sole runtime production-quality exclusion source is
\path{configs/data_quality/pv_excluded_episodes_2025_to_2026_08.csv}, selected by
\path{pv_data_quality.exclusions_csv}. Its explicit reasons include
\texttt{sustained\_zero\_outage}, \texttt{nonzero\_plateau},
\texttt{low\_power} and \texttt{high\_power}. Audit tools generate evidence;
modeling loaders do not run independent zero, plateau or GHI-disagreement
classification rules. For farm $f$, the excluded physical-quarter starts are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal I_f^{\rm excl}
 &=\bigcup_{r:\ f_r=f,\ {\rm decision}_r={\rm exclude}}
       \{t:s_r\le t\le e_r\},\\
 \mathcal D_{f,s}^{\rm model}
 &=\{(x_{d,a,t},P^{\rm AC}_{f,t})\in\mathcal D_{f,s}:
          t\notin\mathcal I_f^{\rm excl}\},
 \qquad s\in\{{\rm train,validation,test}\}.
\end{align}
An excluded quarter is absent from features and targets, rather than retained
with a zero loss weight. Sequence models split into contiguous UTC segments;
they do not bridge a removed interval. Segment weights retain day/auction
weighting. Source records and an independent exclusion audit preserve evidence.

\path{scripts/search_cleaned_pv_forecasts.py} runs at least 16 unique
hyperparameter trials per tunable family, per farm and input regime. The families
are Ridge, Extra Trees, histogram gradient boosting, spline regression, direct
TCN, residual TCN and residual MLP, together with bounded thermal, tracker and
bifacial pvlib fits where applicable. OLS and scale-only pvlib are additional
fixed references. The shared CPU limit is 42 single-threaded workers.

Two paired input regimes use the same removed-instance population. The
operational regime uses cutoff-available forecast weather. The diagnostic
regime replaces forecast GHI with observed satellite GHI while preserving the
forecast direct/global irradiance fraction; Erbs decomposition is used only
when this fraction is undefined. Remaining weather is forecast, not perfect.
Interior satellite gaps are linearly interpolated in physical UTC time within
each split, with no edge extrapolation. Primary scores exclude interpolated
GHI; secondary scores include it.

Models fit 2025, select hyperparameters on January--April 2026 and report
May--August 2026 separately. Retrospective episode curation and earlier
inspection of this report period mean it is a reused curated holdout, not a
new assessment of unseen operating conditions. Metrics are normalized by
installed AC rating. Saved outputs include every trial, family winners,
monthly/auction and daily errors, four local-time periods, seven-day block
uncertainty intervals, and replay-checked standalone model bundles. Operational
model selection uses the forecast-input regime; satellite-input winners are
diagnostic models.

farm and input regime; OLS and scale-only pvlib have one reference fit each.

\section{Optional optimized-imbalance formulation}

This section describes supported code that is disabled in the current farm-910
study. When \texttt{optimize\_imbalances=true}, replace~\eqref{eq:exact} by
\begin{align}
 \delta^+_{s,t}-\delta^-_{s,t}&=g_{s,t}\Delta_t-Q_{s,t},\\
 0\le\delta^+_{s,t}&\le M_t y_{s,t},\\
 0\le\delta^-_{s,t}&\le M_t(1-y_{s,t}),\qquad y_{s,t}\in\{0,1\}.
\end{align}
Here $M_t=(P^{\rm imp}+P^{\rm exp})\Delta_t$, optionally reduced by a configured
imbalance-energy cap. The common $M_t$ is valid because physical and contractual
net energy use the same directional POI range. The sign binary prevents
simultaneous positive/negative deviations, including with negative prices.
Add to each scenario's objective
\begin{equation}
 \sum_t\left[\lambda^+_{s,t}\delta^+_{s,t}
      -\lambda^-_{s,t}\delta^-_{s,t}
      -\kappa(\delta^+_{s,t}+\delta^-_{s,t})\right].
\end{equation}
If this mode has zero settlement price, zero penalty and no useful delivery
restriction, an optimized contract need not be backed by delivered energy.
That is why the present experiment disables optimized deviations and computes
physical mismatches only afterwards. The optional formulation is a mathematical
study mode, not an assertion that deliberate imbalance strategies are permitted.

\section{Data provenance, outputs and reproducibility}

Farm inputs are local read-only Zarr/Parquet stores. API and workbook records use
\path{data/api/market.duckdb}. Raw/normalized snapshots, source identities,
checksums, revision and retrieval metadata support repeated offline studies.
Historical publication remains a separate field/convention. Provider acquisition
is an explicit user-run command; neither the MILP builder nor the historical
experiment downloads missing inputs. DuckDB writers and analysis processes must
be coordinated; complete cached days can be studied before an entire acquisition
window is complete.

YAML controls study date, calendar validity, source mappings, PV and data policies,
BESS and POI characteristics, state, degradation, solver and outputs. One include
is resolved relative to the containing YAML, with recursive mapping overrides.
Every shipped entry point imports \path{configs/standard.yaml} directly;
experiment YAMLs contain only their overrides. The standard exposes shared
feature flags to existing experiments. Explicit null values and replacement
lists survive the merge. The \texttt{resolve-config} command prints the effective
YAML without running a study or accessing provider data.

A single root \path{farm_id}, optionally overridden with \texttt{--farm-id},
selects forecast and physical plant identifiers through typed references resolved
after merging. Each farm uses its own weather and measured-power series and
Parquet metadata for PV geometry and tracking. Automatic directional POI ratings
use declared AC nameplate capacity divided by 1000 as an illustrative MW proxy;
per-farm ratings or explicit plant limits override that rule. Battery size and
SOC percentages remain common, while automatic battery dimensions resolve from
\path{farms.<id>.battery} for the selected physical plant. Numeric experiment
overrides take precedence over these automatic dimensions. The reusable
\path{configs/full_experiments/gme_day.yaml} exposes this workflow.

relative gap, model sizes and timing remain recorded. Before a schedule is used,
independent validation recomputes physical balances, limits, native product
consistency, information sharing, degradation and objective components. Execution
separately checks actual SOC and power/POI protection. Software consistency does
not resolve uncertain farm metadata, forecast bias or historical publication
vintages.

\section{Current limitations and extension boundaries}

The current empirical workflows use daily perfect-price planning with one
weather member, either as independent days or consecutive continuous operation,
with approximate geometry and an idealized interval-average controller.
Measured AC is effective observed production, not an independently estimated
unconstrained irradiance-to-generation potential; hidden plant curtailment/faults
in source production are not identified automatically. Configured cleaning and
fallback choices remain part of the experiment.

The model currently omits continuous trading, order acceptance, market impact,
multiple-farm network coupling, ancillary services, intrainterval dynamics,
ramping/startup constraints, dynamic capacity loss, calendar aging, thermal
battery dynamics, fixed operating costs and investment cash-flow optimization.
The generic \texttt{backtest} CLI remains synthetic-only; the separate historical
\texttt{experiment-gme-oracle}, \texttt{experiment-participation} and
\texttt{experiment-continuous} workflows are the implemented manual-data replays.
Future uncertain-price evaluations need their own causal inputs and comparison
protocol rather than relabeling the current oracle results.

The original implementation-status report is retired. This source and its PDF
are the maintained mathematical description; run manifests and saved solver
results are the numerical evidence for particular configurations. Practical
commands and configuration examples are in the root \path{README.md}.
