\section{Battery wear, control and realized accounting}
\subsection{Linear SOH wear model}
For replacement value $R$, nominal capacity $E_0$, reference cycle depth $d_0$,
cycle life $N$, and committed state of health $h_d$ on delivery day $d$, the
available capacity is $E_d=E_0h_d$. Calendar and cycling capacity losses are
fractions of nominal capacity and accumulate as
\begin{equation}
\Delta q_{\rm cal}=\gamma_{\rm cal}\frac{\Delta t}{T_{\rm yr}},\qquad
\Delta q_{\rm cyc}=\frac{\Delta E_{\rm int}}
{2Nd_0E_0},\qquad
h_{d+1}=1-q_{{\rm cal},d+1}-q_{{\rm cyc},d+1},
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\rm cal}$ is the calendar-loss fraction per year and
$T_{\rm yr}=365\times24$ hours,
$\Delta E_{\rm int}=(\eta_c c+d/\eta_d)\Delta t$ is internal charge-plus-
discharge energy, and $c$ and $d$ are AC charge and discharge powers. The
optimizer uses the current committed SOH for that delivery day, forecasts
losses for future capacity constraints, and commits the updated SOH only at
the end of each chronological day. Forecast damage is priced once as
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm wear}=\frac{R}{1-h_{\rm EOL}}
\left(\Delta q_{\rm cal}+\Delta q_{\rm cyc}\right),
\end{equation}
where $h_{\rm EOL}$ is the end-of-life SOH threshold. This linear cycling
approximation depends on internal throughput, not cycle depth. For the study
calibration, $\gamma_{\rm cal}=0.01\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, $N=6000$, $d_0=1$,
and $h_{\rm EOL}=0.8$; the factor of two converts charge-plus-discharge
energy to equivalent full cycles.
